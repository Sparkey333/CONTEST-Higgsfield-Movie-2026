# MATTER OF LIGHT

**Original screen story by B.L. Barkey · Solo entry · 5:00 · 2.39:1 · 23 shots**

A creator stands on the surface of the Sun and decides to release his brother,
knowing it will probably end everything. A hundred and eight years later a woman
burns her own mind to ash to keep one island hidden. That same sunset two
brothers watch a mountain fall out of the sky — and only one of them learns the
right lesson from it.

---

## The film — three movements, one unbroken string

**I · The Sun.** *80 seconds, 5 shots.* Caedom, a Founder, walks the surface of a
star with Oriane beside him. He intends to release the Vesper — the brother he
sealed away — because a world that cannot be lost is a world that cannot grow.
She asks him to say out loud what that costs. He answers with a date: tomorrow.

**II · The Ocean.** *118 seconds, 9 shots.* A hundred and eight years on. The
Turned come up out of the water at Cairn Island and Oriane meets them alone above
the waves. She wins. Winning takes everything she has — to keep the island off
every map, she burns her own mind to ash.

**III · The Island.** *102 seconds, 9 shots.* The same sunset, from the beach.
Two brothers, eight and six, watch a mountain fall out of the sky and stop. The
younger one cannot look away from the power. The older one wants it as a weapon,
says so, and then — in the last held close-up of the film — puts it down and
finds something better. The film ends on which of them was changed.

## Direction

**Twenty-three shots, not seventy-one.** At four seconds a shot, five minutes
costs seventy-one generations and reads as a sizzle reel. At thirteen seconds a
shot it costs twenty-three and reads as cinema. Mean shot length here is **13.0
seconds**, and every cut is motivated by point of view, time or revelation —
never by a clip running out.

**Three camera grammars, one per movement.**

| Movement | Grammar | What it buys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I · The Sun** | Sustained oner | The first 34 seconds contain no cut at all |
| **II · The Ocean** | Continuous travelling | 118 seconds of action, two point-of-view changes, a climax in total silence |
| **III · The Island** | Locked-off witness | The camera stops moving; one unbroken 14-second wide holds the film's only miracle |

**Power is a system you can read, not a mood.** Escalation runs on two separate
ladders — understanding, which climbs six rungs and stops, and the seventh rung
only a Stone-bearer has. The tell is purely visual: an adept's aura is bright at
the rim and black at the centre, a bearer's is brightest at the centre with no
edge at all. The whole hierarchy is legible in a single frame, and no line of
dialogue is spent explaining it.

**Eighty-nine words.** The screenplay carries 556 words of dialogue. The film
keeps 89 of them — 12 lines across 6 of the 23 shots. Three shots that could have
spoken are silent on purpose, including the climax of Movement II. Four of the
final words are spoken on camera by a boy who has just changed his mind; the two
lines after them are the film's first two lines, said again once they have cost
something.

## Method — everything built here, in this project

**Image-first.** Nothing is generated as video until the film already exists as
stills. Every shot's first and last frame is made as an anchor image, in film
order, and the motion pass is a move between two frames that are already right.
**39 anchor frames** carry all 23 shots, and chained shots share frames across
scene boundaries so a cut physically cannot drift.

**One identity per character, trained once.** Five trained Souls, and **37 locked
reference handles** behind them, drawn from **42 reference sheets** — characters,
lighting plates, locations, props, creatures and effect rule sheets — each
generated on a plain background before a single film frame existed. Damage,
costume and power state are generated *from* that identity rather than
re-described. Re-describing a face is how faces drift.

**Prompts attach; they do not describe.** Every shot prompt names its characters
and props by their reference handles inside the prose and carries an explicit
attach list underneath. A face, a coastline or a creature that already exists is
never re-typed as adjectives.

**One project, three canvases** — one per lighting plate, because every frame on
a board inherits that board's key light. Shots are generated against the plate
they belong to rather than against a description of it.

**Five gates.** Identity, string, timing, motion, picture. Nothing downstream
begins until the gate above it holds, because everything downstream inherits it.

**Two passes, then it ships.** A third pass on the same prompt is almost always a
different prompt, not a better one.

**Deflicker, then upscale, then grade** — in that order.

## Delivery

Every image and video asset is generated inside Higgsfield, in this project,
within the competition window; nothing is carried over from earlier work. All
audio — voice, score and sound design — is AI-generated and uploaded here. No
real person's face or voice is used as an input anywhere in the pipeline. Finish
is 4K, 2.39:1, delivered as MP4. Dialogue and voice-over are in English.
