# SILT

**A DarkHearts Ltd. Short Film**
Written & Directed by B. L. Barkey
Draft 1 · 2026-08-08 · 4 min 22 sec · 61 shots

---

```
                              SILT

                        written & directed by
                           B. L. Barkey


     FADE IN:


     BLACK.

     Not a cut-to-black. Water. Deep, still, and lightless --
     forty feet of it, pressing down.

     A low tone begins under everything. Below hearing, more
     felt than heard. It climbs.

     Somewhere in the green dark, a SHAPE resolves. Curved.
     Furred over with sixty-five years of silt.

     A BELL.

     It hangs motionless. Nothing has touched it.

     It RINGS.

                                                    (ONE STRIKE)

     Deep, bronze, impossibly patient. The note holds, and holds,
     and takes eight full seconds to die.

     TITLE:                    SILT

     Silence.


     HARD CUT TO:


     EXT. CABIN CREEK RESERVOIR -- LATE AFTERNOON

     BLINDING sun.

     A plain of cracked grey-green silt runs to the horizon,
     split into polygonal plates by drought. Bleached tree
     stumps stand in it -- hundreds of them, every one cut off
     flat at exactly the same height, forty feet up.

     Cicadas. Wind. Dust in the air.

     A white county pickup sits at the top of a concrete boat
     ramp. The ramp is painted with lane lines and it runs down,
     and down, and then it simply ends in dirt.

     ELI VANCE, 44, steps down it. Hi-vis orange vest, white hard
     hat, clipboard. He is the only saturated color in the world.

     He stops at the water-level gauge post and looks at it.

     The needle is at the bottom of the scale. There is no more
     scale.

     He writes it down anyway.

     The RADIO on his hip crackles.

                             DISPATCH (V.O.)
                    Cabin Creek, this is Dispatch.
                    Confirming gates open at eighteen
                    hundred. You've got a window.

     Eli thumbs the key without looking at it.

     He checks his watch. 4:41.

     Behind him, upstream, the DAM: a concrete arch sixty-five
     years old, spillway gates shut, turbines humming somewhere
     inside it at a pitch you stop hearing after the first hour.

     He steps off the concrete. His boot goes into the mud.

     First footprint.


     EXT. RESERVOIR BASIN -- CONTINUOUS

     He walks out across the plain.

     He unfolds a SURVEY PLAT. It is old -- 1961, blueprint blue,
     soft at the folds from being unfolded a great many times by
     someone who was not on the clock.

     Printed across the top: REDSTONE, COLO.

     A street grid. Lot numbers. Four hundred people's worth.

     He looks up.

     And there, running through the mud at his feet in a line too
     straight to be anything but poured --

     A CURB.

     He follows it. It turns a corner. Meets another.

     A STREET.

     He walks it.

     Foundation slabs, silt-filled, laid out in rows. A doorway
     standing on its own with nothing on either side of it. A
     MAILBOX on a leaning post, the numbers long gone.

     He checks the plat. His thumb finds a lot number and stays
     there.

     He looks up at a patch of empty mud.

     And then he sees it.


     FOUR CONCRETE STEPS.

     Rising out of the silt. Going up to nothing. Behind them,
     twenty feet back, a fieldstone CHIMNEY, alone.

     That is the whole house. That is all of it.

     Eli stops walking.

     For a long moment he does not do anything at all.

     Then he sits down on the bottom step, and puts the clipboard
     on his knees, and does not write on it.

                                                        (85mm)
     His face. The sun very low and very hard across it.

     Nothing performed. A man sitting on his grandfather's steps
     for the first time, forty feet under a lake that isn't there
     today.

     He checks his watch. 5:38.

     He is fifty-seven minutes past his own schedule.

     He does not move.


     And then --

     A TONE.

     Faint. Almost not there. A single sustained pitch out across
     the plain.

     Eli lifts his head.

     He turns toward the dam, automatically -- machinery, has to
     be machinery --

     It isn't coming from the dam.

     He stands. Turns the other way.


     EXT. RESERVOIR BASIN -- THE TOWER

     Half a mile out on the flats, alone in all that emptiness:

     A STONE TOWER. Forty feet of leached fieldstone, arched
     openings at the top, standing in the middle of nowhere like
     it grew there.

     Eli looks down at his inspection sheet. Runs a finger down
     the hazards column.

                    HAZ-04 -- MASONRY OBSTRUCTION, SUBMERGED
                    LOG EACH INSPECTION. NO ACTION REQUIRED.

     He has written that line every year for eighteen years.

     He has never once seen what was under it.

     He starts walking. Fast.

     The tower grows. At its base he tilts his head back and the
     whole forty feet of it goes up past him -- and there, near
     the top, the pale mineral bathtub ring where the water has
     sat for sixty-five years.

                                                        (85mm)
     His face, tilted up.

     He can hear it clearly now.


     INT. BELL TOWER -- CONTINUOUS

     He ducks through a silt-choked doorway into black.

     HEADLAMP ON.

     A hard cone of light and nothing outside it. The sound is
     bigger in here. Physical. It is in the stone.

     A wooden stair spirals up the inside wall, treads rotted
     black. Above them, iron rungs set into the masonry.

     He climbs.

     Breath. Iron. Wood complaining.

     He comes up into the belfry, and the beam swings across --


     THE BELL.

     Green with patina, caked in silt, enormous at this distance.

     It is not moving.

     It is SINGING.

     Eli puts his bare palm flat against the bronze.

     It is vibrating. Hard enough to feel in the bones of his
     forearm.

     He pulls a handheld analyzer off his belt and holds it up.

                                             82.4 Hz

     He goes very still.

     He turns over the inspection sheet in his other hand -- his
     own sheet, his own handwriting, this morning --

                    TURBINE 2 -- HARMONIC 82.4 Hz -- NOMINAL

     The dam is ringing the bell.

     It has been ringing the bell since 1961. Every day. At the
     same hour. Underwater, where no one could ever hear it.

     There has always been a lake on top of the sound.

     There isn't today.

     The RADIO crackles at his hip.

                             DISPATCH (V.O.)
                    Vance, confirm you're clear of the
                    basin.

     Eli takes the radio off his belt. Thumb over the key.

     Through the arched opening: the dam, small and grey, half a
     mile off. A KLAXON starts somewhere over there.

                             DISPATCH (V.O.) (CONT'D)
                    Gates opening at eighteen hundred.
                    Acknowledge.

     His watch: 5:59.

     He is the engineer of record on this structure. One word
     into that radio and the gates hold for twenty-four hours.
     One word. That is the entire authority of his life and it
     is sitting right there in his hand.

                                                        (85mm)
     His face.

     He has spent forty years being sorry to this valley. Doing
     it perfectly. Filing it correctly. Keeping the thing that
     took his grandfather running smooth as glass, because that
     was the only apology he knew how to make.

     He sets the radio down on the stone sill.

     He does not answer it.

     His hands are empty for the first time in the film.

     He reaches up and takes the ROPE.


     EXT. CABIN CREEK DAM -- SAME

     The klaxon. The gates begin to open.


     INT. BELL TOWER -- CONTINUOUS

     Eli wraps the rope twice around both fists and sets his feet.

     Watch: 6:00.

     He HAULS.

     The headstock groans. Sixty-five years of silt sheets off
     the bronze in a grey avalanche.

     The bell swings --

     -- and the clapper comes home.


                            THE STRIKE.

     Enormous. Bronze. It goes off like a building falling over.
     Silt explodes off the rim in a ring.

     And it is EARLY.

     A beat ahead of the turbine. Not answering the machine --

     Leading it.


     EXT. RESERVOIR BASIN -- CONTINUOUS

     The tower rings out across the whole dead plain. The dam
     sits behind it, grinding its gates open, following.

                                                        (85mm)
     Eli's face in the belfry, lit from below by his own lamp,
     hauling on the rope, ringing it, ringing it.

     He is not crying. He is working.

     For the first time in this film, so is the dam.


     EXT. RESERVOIR BASIN -- MOMENTS LATER

     He walks out across the flats.

     The water is already at his boots. Then his shins. It is
     coming from the left of frame, fast, brown and white and
     absolutely uninterested in him.

     He does not hurry.


     EXT. CABIN CREEK RESERVOIR -- WIDE

     The valley fills.

     Streets go under. The mailbox goes under. The four concrete
     steps go under.

     The tower stands in it, ringing, as the water climbs the
     stone -- thirty feet, thirty-five --

     The sound muffles. Deepens. Slows.

     The belfry goes under.

     And then there is only a lake, flat and bright and ordinary,
     and somewhere down under all of it, on the hour, a bell.


     CARD:

                    REDSTONE, COLORADO
                    FOUNDED 1881 · CONDEMNED 1961
                    SOUNDED DAILY EVER SINCE


     One last strike, forty feet down.

                                                       FADE OUT.

                               END
```

## Notes on the Draft

**Eli never speaks.** Not one line. The only voice in the film belongs to a radio that is not listening. This is load-bearing — the entire arc is *silence → sound*, and giving him dialogue would let him make the sound with his mouth instead of with forty tons of bronze.

**No flashback, no photograph, no 1961.** The four concrete steps are the flashback. If a test audience says they don't understand the grandfather, the fix is one word on the plat — not a scene.

**The dam is not a villain.** It is a machine doing exactly what it was built to do, correctly, forever, with no idea what it's doing. That is the whole point and it is why the ending is not a victory over the dam but a duet with it. Do not let a note session turn this into sabotage.

**The physics is real** — forced harmonic excitation of a structure by rotating machinery. Keep every number defensible. It is the moat.
