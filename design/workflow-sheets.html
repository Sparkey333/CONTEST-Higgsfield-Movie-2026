How a film gets made here

The seventeen steps, in order. ↻ marks where a second pass is expected; ↻↻ where several are and the budget should say so. Grey rows are gates. Date each step when it is genuinely finished — not when it is started.

StepDoneDate
01Decide the film before you open anything
Fix the runtime, the shot count and how many identities appear on screen. Everything downstream derives from these three.
02Create the project
Make it inside the contest or workspace that will verify it, and generate everything there from then on.
03Generate the governing rule sheet ↻
One image that fixes how your world's central visual idea works. It needs no identity and no location, so nothing blocks it.
04Lighting plates, one per movement ↻
Empty frames, no characters. Key direction and palette only.
05Train identity ↻↻
One trained Soul per character. Never two for one person.
06Build the world ↻
Locations, props, creatures, effects. Plain backgrounds, one asset per sheet.
07GATE A — identity lock
Every reference side by side in one view. Look for a face that moved.
08Anchor frames, in film order ↻↻
The first and last frame of every shot, generated as one continuous string rather than per scene.
09GATE B — string lock
Every anchor on one contact sheet, in order. Read it as a comic strip.
10Stills reel
Cut the anchors on a timeline at their real durations and watch the whole thing.
11GATE C — timing lock
Durations are now fixed. Changing one after this point breaks the total runtime.
12Draft motion, cheap tier ↻
Every shot, lowest usable quality, no exceptions and no favourites.
13GATE D — motion lock
Watch it end to end at draft quality. Every camera move and seam is decided here.
14Final shots ↻↻
Highest quality tier, one movement at a time, in film order.
15Finish
Deflicker first, then upscale, then grade. That order, always.
16GATE E — picture lock
No further picture changes. Audio is built against this cut and nothing else.
17Audio, then deliver ↻
Score, dialogue, sound design. Audio is usually exempt from from-scratch rules — check, then reuse freely if it is.
Transfers to the next film unchanged — this page is the method, not the movie
