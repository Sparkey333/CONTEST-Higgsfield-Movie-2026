Power tiers

How power reads on screen, from below the ladder to beyond it. The rungs are Matter of Light's production grammar — its aura grammar, attunement ladder and mind ladder — read against the book, which measures power as understanding and gives no numbered levels ('the source has no levels', film 1's asset register). Book lines carry their chapter; [Matter of Light: …] marks a film-1 reference; anything neither says is marked as a design choice. A shot names its rungs in shots[].tiers as 'who:tier'.

  • Read the rung from the light, not from effects: where the light sits in the body, how hard its edge is, and what it costs to look at. 'Much suffering was required to reach the level capable of even looking upon one of the Seven Stones' (Ch 01).
  • An adept's light is bright at the rim and black at the centre; a bearer's is brightest at the centre with no edge [Matter of Light: aura-grammar]. Episode 2 adds a third: a Prison's light is a bearer's core held behind plates of dead stone.
  • Colour belongs to the Stone, not the rung: Voids is violet and Dominion emerald (Ch 11). Film 1 soured its adepts toward bruise violet and made the Stone of Voids green-white [Matter of Light: ladder-attunement, founding-stone]; from Episode 2 an adept's light goes cold toward steel grey-blue instead, so violet means Voids alone (design choice, from Ch 11). Stardust's light stays white; the violet of Movement I is the sky around it.
  • Each rung up costs something you can see. The mind buys 'perception of the present, while limiting her foresight' (Ch 02): above sixteen, every split takes colour or sound away.
  • States are prompt lines, not new elements: attach the character's element and add the rung's prompt line from this table.
  • The content filter refuses body-part wording on a breaking or assembled figure ('collarbone to hip', 'stitched from stolen parts', 'writhing'); write the figure, the seam and the light instead. Both refusals cost nothing.
