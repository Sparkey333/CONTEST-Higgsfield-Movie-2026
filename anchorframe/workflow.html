Nine stages. Five gates. One loop.

Nothing goes to video until it already exists as stills that are right. Every face is attached by id, never re-described. Every take is on a ledger. The cut is chosen from evidence.

MATTER OF LIGHT — 5:43, 21:9, 27 shots, made entirely in Higgsfield for the Higgsfield Global Film Festival, Aug 8 – Sep 14 2026. Every number below is that production's, not an estimate.

STAGE 0

Story

What is the film about, and who changes?

A logline, a screenplay, a shot list whose durations sum to the target, and a dialogue cut small enough to be spoken by a machine and believed.

Do

  1. Write the logline first; if it needs a second sentence the film has two subjects.
  2. Cut the screenplay to shots at cinema length (mean 13s), not sizzle length (4s). Motivate every cut by point of view, time or revelation.
  3. Cut dialogue to what a synthetic voice can carry: 556 → 89 words here. Mark the shots that are silent on purpose.
  4. Name the turn. The last held close-up belongs to whoever changes.

In

  • source text or premise
  • runtime target
  • the rules of the venue (minimum length, format, what must be made inside the platform)

Out

  • logline (≤140 chars, three variants)
  • screenplay
  • shot list with durations and acts
  • dialogue cut (lines per shot, words per second budget)
  • the turn — which character changes and in which shot

STAGE 1

Cast

Does every face, place and thing exist once, by id?

Every character, lighting plate, location, prop, creature and effect generated on a plain background inside the project, each an element with a uuid, each Soul trained once. Nothing downstream describes a face; it attaches one.

Do

  1. Rule sheets before any character (the aura rule; the attunement ladder).
  2. Leads: train a Soul, then generate state sheets from it (damaged, ascended, mortal) — never re-describe the face.
  3. Plates: one per act plus one for any lighting exception (the bombardment). Every frame inherits its board's key.
  4. Locations, props, creatures, effects: plain background, three views on one sheet where it helps.
  5. Record every id. Reference as @[name](uuid) in every prompt from here on.

In

  • shot list
  • the world's rules written as sheets (here: the aura hierarchy — bright rim/black centre for adepts, bright centre/no edge for bearers)

Out

  • cast: characters / environments / props / fx as name → uuid
  • souls: one per lead
  • retired: old name → old uuid, so old renders still attribute
  • one Canvas board per lighting plate

STAGE 2

Anchors

Does the film exist as stills, in order, that are already right?

Two frames per shot — first and last — generated in film order on the right board, chained from the previous frame. Shared frames end one shot and start the next; bridge frames are near-black hidden cuts; match pairs are two images designed together.

Do

  1. Generate F01 first: the film's first frame should be almost nothing.
  2. Chain: each frame references the previous frame's job id, not its prompt.
  3. One image doing two jobs (shared) is generated once. A bridge must go genuinely near-black. A match pair is made as a pair.
  4. No text, no watermark, no logo in any frame — say it three ways in the prompt.

In

  • cast ids
  • shot list
  • boards

Out

  • anchor plan: F01…Fnn with role (single / shared / bridge / match), handles, dependency, job id, url
  • the motion sheet

STAGE 3

Stills reel & timing

Does it play, at length, before anything moves?

Assemble the anchors at their shot durations and watch the whole runtime as stills. Then freeze runtime, order and count — after this a structural change costs video, not images.

Do

  1. Cut the stills at duration. Sit through it. Where attention drops is where a shot is too long or a cut is unmotivated.
  2. Decide the structure now: linear, prologue splice, fold, interlace. Price each in new anchors.
  3. Freeze: runtime, shot order, shot count.

In

  • anchors
  • shot durations

Out

  • the stills reel
  • the structural decision (which ordering)
  • timing lock

STAGE 4

Motion

Is every shot a move between two frames that are already right?

Video for every shot, generated inside the project, two takes each, on the model whose ceiling, reference support and audio behaviour fit the shot. Chain start and end frames on anything over 12 seconds.

Do

  1. Read what you are actually using before you spec a run — pull the project's placements and its history. (The notes said v2 · 12s · 720p. The account said v4.0 · 16s.)
  2. Cheap pass on every shot first; finals per act after.
  3. Two takes per prompt. A third take on the same prompt is a different prompt.
  4. Audio off on the shots the shot list marks silent. Audio references, not model audio, wherever there is dialogue.
  5. Every take through the Predictor. Read the curve, not the number.

In

  • anchors
  • cast ids
  • the model matrix (duration ceiling · two-frame · audio refs · aspect · folder)

Out

  • takes, admitted to the project by cast
  • scores for every take ≤16s
  • picks and alternates per shot

STAGE 5

Voice & sound

Is every sound in the project, and is every silence a decision?

Dialogue generated per line, several takes each, chosen for the scene's mood and routed into the shots as audio references. Score and sound design made wherever is honest, then every file uploaded into the project.

Do

  1. Cast two candidates per character on the same line; pick; write it down.
  2. Four takes per line with mood settings from the scene. Keep the spec take as take 1.
  3. Route: generate the voice first, attach it as an audio reference on a model that accepts one, turn the model's own audio off on that shot.
  4. Credit music honestly on the end card. Keep the rights receipt.

In

  • dialogue cut
  • voice casting
  • the scene moods

Out

  • takes per line with settings
  • selected take per line
  • music and sound design files
  • every audio file in the submission project

STAGE 6

The cut

Which take, in what order, and why?

The board: one pick per shot, alternates beside it, the continuance from first shot to last. Cut on the tail, never the head. Selection is the grade. Fix structure with edits before generations. End-card plate generated; end-card type in the NLE.

Do

  1. Decide the rhythm before you pick: held frames or montage to a song. A board built for holds cannot supply a montage; the editor will reach past it for coverage.
  2. Pick by look, tie-break by score; write why under each pick.
  3. Trim from the head. 17 of 18 takes paid on their last second.
  4. Where a chroma take and a flat take exist, the chroma take is the grade. No colour pass across two lighting worlds in an hour.
  5. Timeline resolution = the film's aspect before export, or the bars bake in. 2560×1098 · 24 · H.264 CRF 17 · AAC 320.
  6. End card: generate the plate with no text (say it three ways); lay the credits in the NLE at 12s+.

In

  • takes + scores
  • the shot list
  • voice takes

Out

  • board.html
  • pull-picks.sh — the picks in cut order + a rough assembly
  • the export

STAGE 7

Deliver

Does it count?

Rights pass, watermark and packshot from inside the platform, a public post on a public account, every file verified inside the project, submitted before the deadline in the deadline's own timezone.

Do

  1. Rights: end card credits the source and the AI audio honestly; no real face or voice anywhere in the inputs.
  2. MVS 1 — watermark + packshot applied by the platform's own flow, never recreated.
  3. MVS 2 — public post, public account, checked in a private window.
  4. Verify every generation is inside the project. Submit. Then stop.
  5. Deadline in its own timezone. Two files in this repo disagreed (UTC vs PT). Work to the earlier one.

In

  • the export
  • the rules, read again today

Out

  • the watermarked file
  • the public post (and its logged-out check)
  • the submission
  • posts and descriptions for X / Instagram / YouTube
  • a support email on file if anything errored
  • the published project page — film, prompts and history, public

STAGE 8

Learn

What did the cut change, and what does the next film inherit?

After delivery, put the finished film back through the desk: upload the master into the project root, run the scene analysis, diff the scenes against the board as it stood at picture lock, and write every delta as a lesson the next project starts with. The board is the plan; the cut is the truth; the difference between them is the course.

Do

  1. Upload the master into the project root and keep the low proxy beside it. The analyser takes either; the jury took the master.
  2. Start the scene analysis, then do not wait for it: read the favourites against the picks first. A favourite on a take that is not the pick is the cut telling you where the board was wrong.
  3. Diff in five columns: order, durations, take swaps, trims, drops and additions. Price every delta — an edit, or a generation?
  4. Read the feel and the grade off the scenes: where the light drifts between neighbours, where a hold ran long, where a cut was unmotivated.
  5. Write each delta three ways: a lesson (what happened → the rule now), a reminder on the stage that could have caught it, and an example the next project sees.

In

  • the delivered master, and the low proxy beside it
  • board.html as it stood at picture lock
  • the post-deadline generations, if any
  • the favourites in the project — the cut's own record of what it used

Out

  • final-cut.data.json — plans, post-deadline work, favourites, uploads, analyses
  • final-cut.notes.json — scenes, the diff, feel, grade, lessons
  • new reminders on the stages that could have caught each delta
  • the examples the next project sees under Cut and Learn

WHYWhat is different about this

Continuity by id

Faces, places and things are attached, never described. A rename cannot break a reference; a retired element is recorded, not forgotten.

Provenance you can hand to a jury

Every take the desk makes is on a ledger before anyone waits for it. Admission to a project is by cast, not by folder — which is the only thing the API can actually enforce.

Evidence, then taste

Every take scored; the curves read against what a jury sits there to see. The cut is chosen with numbers on the table and a person making the call.

Gates are people

Five gates, each a person looking at a screen, each recorded. Nothing downstream begins until the gate above holds.
