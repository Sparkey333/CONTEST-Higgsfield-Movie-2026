03Start a project

01

Copy the example

cp anchorframe/project.example.json anchorframe/projects/<slug>/project.json

Title, kind, format, the Higgsfield project URL and folder id, the date window.

02

Write the cast as ids

Characters, environments, props, effects — name → element uuid. This list is what makes a generation yours. Souls one per name.

03

Write the shots

Id, act, title, duration, audio on or off, and the prompt with @[name](uuid) references. Leave pick empty; the board chooses by score until you decide.

04

Ingest the history

python3 anchorframe/ingest.py anchorframe/projects/<slug> <dump> [...]

Dumps are the files the Higgsfield connector writes when history is paged in Claude. Anything not made with your cast is dropped and counted.

05

Build and open

python3 anchorframe/build.py anchorframe/projects/<slug> · python3 anchorframe/desk.py · anchorframe/serve.command. Clips play inline when served or opened locally.

06

Keys, then generate

Export keys.env from the keys page. python3 anchorframe/generate.py anchorframe/projects/<slug> --shot S5 --model <cloud model id> --prompt-from-shot. The request id lands on the ledger on acceptance.

04The loop

The inner loop that runs inside stages 4–6, and once more, after delivery, in stage 8 until the picks are the film.

1 · ingest

Admit this project's takes — by cast id, by ledger, by hand map. Drop and count the rest.

ingest.py <slug> dump…
2 · score

Run every take ≤16s through the Virality Predictor, two at a time. Free.

ingest.py … --scores scores.json
3 · build

Lay the board. Pick by your call, else by score, else newest.

build.py <slug>
4 · choose

Look, then write pick + alternates into the shot, with why.

project.json → shots[]
5 · generate

Make only what is missing or broken. Ledger on acceptance.

generate.py … --prompt-from-shot
6 · deliver

Pull the picks in cut order. Native size ×2 at most. Titles in the NLE.

board.html → open the clip

05One folder, and what that can mean

The desk is exclusive to one Higgsfield project. It enforces that the only way the API allows: admission by cast (a take is yours if it was made with an element in your cast), ledger on submission (the desk records what it makes before it waits), and a hand map for anything you place yourself.

Folder placement itself stays in the web app. No image model and almost no video model accepts a folder id over the API. When a rule needs the generation history to live inside the project, generate there in Cinema Studio; the desk will find those renders by their cast and lay them out.

History pulls come through the Higgsfield connector in Claude, which pages the account and writes files. Point ingest.py at them.

07What is different about this

Continuity by id

Faces, places and things are attached, never described. A rename cannot break a reference; a retired element is recorded, not forgotten.

Provenance you can hand to a jury

Every take the desk makes is on a ledger before anyone waits for it. Admission to a project is by cast, not by folder — which is the only thing the API can actually enforce.

Evidence, then taste

Every take scored; the curves read against what a jury sits there to see. The cut is chosen with numbers on the table and a person making the call.

Gates are people

Five gates, each a person looking at a screen, each recorded. Nothing downstream begins until the gate above holds.

09Working tips

What one production learned, in the order you meet it. Source: tips.md.

Before the first frame

  1. One project, one cast, ids not names. Every character, location, prop and effect is an element with a uuid, referenced as @[name](uuid). A rename cannot break an id. A prompt that describes a face instead of attaching one is a prompt that will drift.
  2. Build the cast on plain backgrounds first. Damage, costume and power state are generated from the identity, never re-described. Re-describing a face is how faces drift.
  3. Retire elements deliberately. When you replace one (prop_enemy_ships → twelve-ships), record old id → new id in project.json → retired and sweep every prompt. Two swaps went unrecorded on this film and would have shipped four prompts pointing at dead elements.
  4. One lighting plate per act, one board per plate. Every frame on a board inherits that board's key. A board that mixes plates is a board where the light drifts.

Generating

  1. Image first. Nothing goes to video until the film exists as stills. A shot is a move between two frames that are already right.
  2. Chain from the rendered last frame, never from the anchor still. The still is the target; the render is the truth. Only 2 of 16 renders on this film used start/end frames — both were the longest shots, which is exactly right. Chain anything over 12s.
  3. Two takes, then rewrite. A third take on the same prompt is almost always a different prompt, not a better one.
  4. Read what you are actually using. The desk's own notes said v2, 12s, 720p. The account said v4.0, 16s. Pull the history before you spec a run; ingest.py exists for this.
  5. Hold the long shots whole. Awe needs duration. The Predictor curves say the same thing the bible did: attention is still climbing at the last frame of every held wide.

Scoring and choosing

  1. Score every take, then read the curves, not the number. 17 of 18 renders peaked on their final second. That is the film working — and it is why the hook column is weak: the tool scores seconds 0–3 and this kind of film does not front-load.
  2. Use the hook score for one thing: the public post. Cut the post peak-first from the three highest-rated final seconds. The film's opening can be quiet by design; the post's cannot.
  3. Cut on the tail, never the head. If a take is longer than its slot, the time comes off the front.
  4. Selection is the grade. Same shot, five takes: the two chroma takes scored 59 and 58; the flat ones 49. One look decision, ten points — and a colour pass across two lighting worlds in an hour produces drift, which a cinematographer punishes. Pick the graded take; ship the renders as rendered.
  5. Watch for the identity break the model can't see. The Predictor scored S5 fine. S5 had the wrong Caedom sheet in all four takes. Scores rank takes; only the continuity audit catches the wrong face.

Sound

  1. Route dialogue through a model that takes audio references (minimax_h3 was the one here that also chained frames at 21:9). Generate the voice first, attach it, and turn the model's own audio off on that shot.
  2. Silence is a choice you make once. Decide which shots are silent in the shot list, and set audio: false there, so nobody turns ambience on by habit.
  3. The auditory channel was the weakest region in 13 of 18 clips. Generated ambience is doing less than you think. Where speech arrives, attention jumps with it — put the film's first line under its first three seconds.

Delivering

  1. Deliver at the render's native size ×2 at most. 1344×576 to UHD is 8× the pixels and produces a soft image with 4K in the filename. Up to 4K is a ceiling, not a floor.
  2. Timeline resolution before export. A 16:9 timeline with 21:9 clips bakes the bars in. Set the timeline to the film's aspect first, then export.
  3. End card plate and end card type are two things. Generate the plate (no text, no letters, no numerals in the prompt — say so three ways). Lay the type in the NLE. Every model garbles letterforms, and a misspelled author credit is the worst possible last frame.
  4. Watermark, packshot, public post, public account. Whatever the rules name as the minimal submission, do it in the platform's own flow and check every link in a private window. A private account is the most common way a strong entry becomes an ineligible one.

The desk itself

  1. Every submission goes on the ledger before you wait for it. generate.py writes the request id to project.json on acceptance. If the wait dies, the id survives.
  2. Keys in keys.env, never in a prompt, never in a commit. The keys page exports to a gitignored filename on purpose.
  3. Regenerate, don't hand-edit. board.html is a function of three JSON files. If it is wrong, the JSON is wrong.

After the cut

  1. The board is the plan; the cut is the truth; the difference is the course. Put the delivered film back through the desk. Upload the master into the project root, run the scene analysis, diff the scenes against the board as it stood at picture lock, and write each delta as a lesson, a reminder on the stage that could have caught it, and an example. Stage 8 exists for this.
  2. Favourites are the cut's own record. Read them against the picks before any analyser returns: a favourite on a take that is not the pick is the editor telling you where the board was wrong. On this film seven of the favourited takes were the board's alternates, and every one was a look decision, not a score decision.
  3. A 16-second render is 16.1 seconds. The Predictor refuses it. Score the 15s and 8s takes; for the 16s ones trim a quarter-second off the tail before you upload, or accept that the longest shots go unscored.
  4. Regenerate silent shots with audio off, and solo the tail of every audio-on take. Two of four 'regenerate this clip' takes made on deadline night came back with the model's own voice — "Thank you" at 14–15s in one, "I'm sorry." at 12–13s in the other — in flank runs with no dialogue. Phantom speech is inaudible in a browser tile and very audible in a cinema.
  5. The analyser queues, and it is slow on a six-minute film — 59 minutes to leave the queue on this one, and it labels a film's scenes with a marketing vocabulary ('Opening Hook', 'Usage Scenarios') you ignore. Its scene splits, palettes and audio transcriptions are the useful part. Start it the moment the master exists, and do the favourites diff by hand while it runs.
  6. Keep the master and the low proxy as two uploads in the project root. The proxy is what you analyse and share; the master is what the jury saw. Name both in project.json → final so the next reader knows which is which.
  7. Plan coverage or plan montage, but decide. The board planned 23 shots at 13 seconds; the cut ran 100 at 3.7, because one generation per shot gave the editor one angle and the film wanted three. If the film is to hold, generate the B lane and the multi-shot lane for every dialogue and set-piece shot before Gate D. If it is to be cut to music, write the board for it.
  8. Lock the song before the stills reel. Three songs scored this film and the cuts land on their lyrics; the silent climax did not survive 'In the sky!' over the eye of the storm. The bible's own music-video rule applied and nobody noticed it applying.
  9. Audit the timeline, not the board. Five costume states of the lead and two different beasts for one creature reached the jury. The continuity audit ran on the board, where every pick was clean; the seams only exist on the timeline. Run it at Gate E, before sound.
  10. Read every wide back through the analyser before delivery. It described the reef swimmers as toddlers and the beach runners as young boys. The sheets say nineteen and eighteen; a wide does not carry the age, and a juror reads the wide.

The next film

  1. A sequel starts from ids, not descriptions. The previous film's plates, locations and Souls are attached by their old ids; the first shot of the new film can stand in the last place of the old one on the same two elements, and the join is a fact. Write the new faces as one-line sheet briefs in project.json → sheets so Stage 1 runs from the file.
  2. Write the lessons into the board before Gate A. inherits.lessons_applied is where the last Learn stage becomes this film's rules: shot ceilings, audio off on silent shots, the B lane named per set piece, the song locked, every sheet 18+. A lesson that is only in a chapter is a lesson that will be relearned.
