0. HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

This is the "Background" upload for Higgsfield's script tools. Everything below

================================================================
1. THE FILM IN ONE PARAGRAPH
================================================================
In a coastal town the sea withdraws for exactly six minutes each day. A bell
rings, the water pulls back, and miles of glistening seabed are exposed —
wrecks, stranded things, a whole hidden landscape. Everyone stays behind the
line. A boy does not. He runs out across the seabed to retrieve something.
He reaches it and turns; the water is already returning. The run back is the
action sequence. He makes it. What he was carrying is revealed: something
small, and worth it.

Runtime target: 3:45. Hard floor 3:00, measured on the delivered file.
Register: kinetic, ticking clock, breathless — then still.

================================================================
2. THE LOCKS — these are decided, not open questions
================================================================
THE EMBER (the feeling at the last frame)
  The ache of having risked everything for something only you understand the
  value of. Not triumph. Recognition.

THE TURN (one reversal, lands at 60-70% of runtime, approx. 2:20-2:40)
  True at 0:15: the seabed is a place you can cross and come back from.
  False by the end: he only just makes it, and the margin was never his.
  The turn is the moment he TURNS AROUND and sees the water already moving.

THE RULE (established by 0:15, no preamble)
  Bell rings. Sea withdraws. Six minutes. Everyone stays behind the line.
  This must be legible with no dialogue and no title card.

THE ENDING
  The last frame recontextualises the opening. What he retrieved re-writes why
  he ran. Reveal it late — the film is shot AROUND this fact for three minutes.

THE SUBJECT — identity invariants (must survive every shot)
  One boy, roughly 10 years old. Non-negotiable across all shots:
  - a red-brown oilskin coat, too big for him, sleeves rolled
  - bare feet, always
  - dark hair, always wet
  - a canvas satchel worn across the body, left shoulder to right hip
  - he never smiles until the final shot
  Deliberately variable: exact hair length, background townspeople, cloud cover.
  Silhouette test: readable in pure black at thumbnail size. The coat shape and
  the satchel strap are the silhouette.

THE WORLD — palette and light logic
  Palette, four values only:
  - wet-sand silver     #C9C3B4
  - low-sun amber       #E0A356
  - deep tidal blue     #24404F
  - near-black rock     #14100E
  Light logic: ONE source. Low sun, raking, from frame-left, near the horizon.
  Long shadows across wet sand. Mirror reflections in standing water.
  THE SHIFT: the palette cools hard at the turn — amber drains out, tidal blue
  takes the frame — and returns to amber only in the final shot.
  Texture floor: fine grain, slight halation on the sun. Keep it in every shot.
  FORBIDDEN: teal-orange grading, lens flares, colour that is not in the four.

THE GRAMMAR — what the camera may do
  Lens set: 35mm for the town, 85mm compression for the run (the water reads
  closer and faster on a long lens — this is the point).
  Height: the boy's eye height in the town. Ground level on the seabed.
  Permitted: locked-off wides, one slow push per act, tracking ONLY during the
  run back.
  THE PROHIBITION: no handheld, ever. No drifting camera on static beats.
  The camera is still until the water moves. That contrast is the film.

THE BEATS — approximate budget, 3:45 total
  0:00-0:15  The bell. The sea withdraws. The rule, established.
  0:15-0:45  The line. The town behind it. The boy at the edge.
  0:45-1:10  He crosses the line. Nobody stops him.
  1:10-2:20  The seabed. The hidden landscape. He finds it.
  2:20-2:40  THE TURN. He turns. The water is already coming.
  2:40-3:25  The run back. The action sequence.
  3:25-3:45  He makes it. The reveal. The last frame.

SOUND
  Sound carries this film. The bell. Water withdrawing (a vast inhale).
  Bare feet on wet sand. Breath. Then the returning water as a low wall of
  pressure, not a crash.
  ONE DELIBERATE SILENCE: the moment he turns. Everything drops out.
  Music: original composition or royalty-free ONLY. Licence must be held.

3. WHAT A SCRIPT FOR THIS FILM IS
================================================================
THIS FILM HAS NO DIALOGUE AND NO NARRATION. None. Not a voiceover, not a
title card, not a whispered line at the end.

A valid script for it is:
  - a numbered shot list
  - each shot: duration in seconds, framing, lens, what is in frame, what MOVES
  - a parallel sound column
  - continuity note: what must match the previous shot

An INVALID script for it is:
  - a hook line, a narrator, a "here's why this matters" structure
  - anything that explains the premise in words
  - anything that states the theme aloud
  - a listicle, an essay, a retention-optimised talking-head format

If the framework you are using requires narration, it is the wrong framework
for this film. Say so rather than producing narration.

================================================================
4. HARD BANS — violating any of these scores zero in every criterion
================================================================
  - No copyrighted IP, no recognisable movie characters, no brand logos.
    Generated coastal towns hallucinate signage and boat names. Watch for it.
  - No licensed music. Original or royalty-free only.
  - No NSFW.
  - NO POLITICAL STATEMENT. No real nations, flags, borders, conflicts,
    elections, uniforms that read as a real force. The town is invented and
    unplaceable. It is not a real coast.
  - NO RELIGIOUS STATEMENT. The bell is a civic bell, not a church bell.
    No crosses, no spires, no prayer postures, no afterlife reading.
    THIS IS A REAL RISK: a coastal town with a bell tower defaults to a church.
    It must not.
  - Runtime >= 3:00 on the delivered file.
  - Official watermark required. Public post required. Prompts and generation
    history must be published.

================================================================
5. KNOWN PRODUCTION RISKS
================================================================
  - Returning-tide water is the hardest generation in the film. It is
    UNTESTED. If it cannot be made to hold, the concept pivots — do not write
    around a shot that does not exist yet.
  - Exteriors mean light and weather continuity across many shots. The single
    low-sun source is what protects this.
  - A running child is far easier to hold consistent than a face in dialogue.
    That is a deliberate design choice, not an accident.

6. WHAT WE ARE OPTIMISING
================================================================
Judged by three people who want different films:
  - Edwin Catmull (Pixar co-founder) wants HEART.
  - Phedon Papamichael (cinematographer, Nebraska) wants CRAFT and light.
  - Paul W. S. Anderson (Mortal Kombat, Event Horizon) wants PROPULSION.
Scoring: Cinematic 25 / Story 25 / Technical 20 / Platform engagement 15 /
Social engagement 15.

The film must carry feeling THROUGH momentum. A beautiful static short loses
one of three votes outright.
