Correction to an earlier rule. “No scene folders anywhere” was too broad. It holds for references — an identity split across scenes destroys the one view where drift is visible. It does not hold for shots: grouping those by movement is the position axis, and it is the same thing the anchor folders do. So keep Scene #1/#2/#3, rename them MV1 · MV2 · MV3 to match the film, and put only shots and anchors in them. Never a character.

Retention the question that costs most to get wrong

Never
delete

Old and bad generations stay

The entry is verified against the project's own generation history. Deleting weakens that record and buys you nothing — storage is not your constraint, provenance is.

Move them, don't bin them. Straight to 99 · LOOKDEV with the original generation name untouched. Renaming a reject wastes the only thing it is still good for: proving what you tried.

Do

Merge the duplicate handles first

@Oriane, @Oriane-1 and @Oriane-d… are one identity in three places — the exact condition that makes a face drift. Consolidate to @Oriane; her damaged and ascended states are filename states, not separate handles.

Same for the three unnamed Characters folders: 27 generations currently invisible at Gate A.

Downloading favourites yes — for three reasons, not four

Do

Download the keeper only

Full resolution, one per asset. Three legitimate uses: the revision loop (you cannot get a v2 without showing the v1), offline contact sheets at Gate A and Gate B, and backup against an account problem three weeks before deadline.

Never

Re-upload a download into the film

A downloaded frame pushed back in arrives as an import, not a generation, and its history no longer sits in the project. Regenerate from the same Soul instead. Downloads are for looking at.

Also skip downloading rejects — they are useful in the project and useless on your disk.

Canvas and the other scratch surfaces

Worth it for

  • Comparing many variants spatially, which a vertical feed is bad at
  • Mood and palette thinking before a plate exists
  • Rough layout of a frame you will then generate properly

Costs you

  • Provenance — anything composed on a side surface risks landing outside the project's generation history
  • A second place for assets to live, which is how the naming discipline quietly dies
Ruling: lookdev only. Think on it freely, then regenerate the decision inside the project and park the sketch in 99 · LOOKDEV. If a frame's history is not in the project, that frame cannot be in the film. Verify once before relying on it — compose one throwaway on Canvas and check whether it appears in the project's history.

The four tools two execute the plan, two compete with it

Core

Elements

The @handle library. This is the single most important tool in the room — it is what keeps a face the same across nine weeks. Everything in the roster below lives here.

Core

The generate bar

Model, references, aspect, resolution, duration. Where the work actually happens. Check the settings every session — see the warning below.

Limited

Canvas

Lookdev only. Compare variants spatially, think, then regenerate the decision inside the project. Nothing that ships gets composed on it.

Limited

Chat & AI Director

These generate decisions — shot ideas, directions, structure. You already have those, written down and argued for. Using them now means negotiating your own plan with a tool that has not read the novel.

The one good use: a second opinion at a gate. Ask AI Director what it would do with a shot you are stuck on, take the idea if it is better, and never let it re-plan the film.

The generate bar is currently set to 4:3, 30s. The film is 21:9 and its longest shot is 18 seconds. Those two settings will quietly produce unusable frames at full price — reset them to 21:9 and the shot's real duration before the next generation, and check them at the top of every session.

Elements the handle is an API — treat it like one

Never

Put the version in the handle

@oriane_v5, @char_oriane_v3, @char_oriane_v4 — you call these by name inside a prompt, so a handle that says v5 tells you nothing about what you are calling. You end up opening thumbnails to remember, every single time.

A handle is a name, not a changelog. The moment you write a prompt using it, renaming it breaks that prompt.

Do

The handle encodes state

@oriane_rest · @oriane_ascended · @oriane_damaged. Now the prompt reads itself, and the handle never has to change again for the life of the production.

Keep the display name as the character — Oriane — so the library still groups them. That part you already have right.

Never

Mix two states inside one element

This is the one that actually causes drift. An element holding both cream-mantle and dark-robe images averages them on every call, and you get a costume that exists in neither. One state per element, always.

Rule

An element earns its existence at two shots

Appears in two or more shots → it is an element. Appears once → it is a prompt. Ten elements is a healthy roster for a five-minute film; thirty is a library you will stop maintaining by week three.

Improving an element the answer to “add or rename?”

Add

Better images go into the existing element

Generate a stronger frame → add it to the same element and remove the weakest one it beats. The handle stays; every prompt you have already written keeps working; the element quietly gets better.

Cap it around twelve. An element is a curated set, not an archive — the archive is 99 · LOOKDEV.

New

A new element only for a genuinely new state

Different costume, different power rung, different age. Not “a better version” — that is an add. If you cannot name the new state in one word, it is not a new element.

What you chose, not just what you made. Leave the filter chips visible.

04

Model, ratio, duration. Ten seconds to take, and it catches wrong settings — it was last seen on 4:3 at 30s for a 21:9 film.

Then

One line of text

Where you are, what you did since last time, what is blocking you. Three sentences maximum — the screenshots carry the facts, the line carries the intent.

The desk builds this for you. index.html → Status read holds the same four captures as a checklist, takes the images, and generates the hand-off block with the counts already in it — then logs the reading so the day-over-day delta is visible instead of remembered.
Three rules: full window, never a crop. One screen per image, never a collage. Never scale down — the counts stop being legible.

What comes back: an updated slot count, the next three moves in order, and anything in the screenshots that contradicts the plan — a stray aspect ratio, a handle that split, a folder filling up that should not be.

Verification sequence run in this order — each step's input is the one above

  1. Rebuild the handles around stateNine elements, one state each, named for what they are: @oriane_rest, @oriane_ascended, @oriane_damaged, @caedom_ascended, @caedom_mortal, @alder, @wren, @threadwright, @vigil. Retire the _v3 _v4 _v5 handles as you go — nothing downstream references them yet, which is exactly why now is the cheap moment.
  2. Sort every existing generation into one stateYour seven current character elements are versions of two people. Decide, per image, which state it belongs to and put it there. Anything that fits no state is lookdev, not an element.
  3. Favourite the keepers, inside the projectFavourite is your working filter — it is how you find a frame again in a library of three hundred. Favourite the ones going into elements, and nothing else.
  4. Load each element to 8–12 imagesVaried angle, framing and light, all of one state. Add the strongest, drop the weakest it beats. Stop at twelve.
  5. Download one contact set per elementFull resolution, keepers only. This is what you bring back for a refinement pass — the prompt cannot be improved without seeing what it produced.
  6. Bring them here with a drift reportAttach the images and say what held, what drifted, which of the four was worst and what must not change. You get back a v2 prompt with the changed clauses called out.
  7. Regenerate against the same elementPaste the v2 and call the same @handle. Never retrain to fix a prompt problem — that moves the face, which is the thing the element exists to hold still. Good results get added to the element; the handle never changes.
  8. Rename everything from the ledgerEvery keeper gets its full filename now, not later from memory. generation_4471 is unfindable in nine weeks; REF-oriane-A7-M016-B-03 is not.
  9. Sweep the rejects into 99 · LOOKDEVNames untouched. The folder should be the largest in the project and that is correct.
  10. Tick the binding board116 slots. Every one you can tick is done; every one you cannot is the actual to-do list. This replaces the feeling of being behind with a number.
  11. Build the Gate A contact sheetEvery reference at the same size in one view. You are looking for one thing: a face that moved between sheets.
  12. GATE A — identity lockNothing downstream begins until this holds, because everything downstream inherits it. A failure here is cheap; the same failure found at Gate D costs thirty-nine anchors.
  13. Generate anchors as one stringFilm order, not scene order. This is where the most iteration lives — budget for it rather than being surprised by it.
  14. GATE B — string lockAll 39 anchors on one sheet, read as a comic strip. If the story does not work here it will not work in motion, and motion costs ten times more.
  15. Cut the stills reelAnchors on a timeline at real durations. Nearly free, and it finds pacing problems a contact sheet hides completely.
Where you are right now: roughly 292 generations exist and the film has 116 slots. That gap is not waste — it is the search. The next move is not more generating; it is naming what you already have so the board can tell you what is genuinely missing.
