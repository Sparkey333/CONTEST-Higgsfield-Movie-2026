How a board works

Five rules. They are the whole method; everything below is these five applied to 39 anchors and 23 shots.

01
One canvas per plate, not per scene. Movement I's coda returns to the Sun, so S23 sits on Canvas I even though it plays last. Filing by light rather than by running order is what stops the grade drifting between boards.
02
Top row is the shelf: plate, then location, then people and props. Dragged in from the Elements panel by handle, never described in words. Each board lists its own shelf below — that chip row is the drag order.
03
Anchors left to right in shot order, shots drawn underneath. A shared frame ends one shot and starts the next; it is one tile serving two spans, so it gets generated once. A bridge is a near-black hidden cut. A match pair is two different images designed together.
04
Chain from the rendered last frame, never from the anchor still. Once shot N exists, pull its final frame and start shot N+1 from that. The anchor is the target; the render is the truth.
05
Spend the allowance where the film is decided. F35 first, then F06/F07, F17, and the F09/F10 match. The API made one of each; the canvas makes the tenth. Any face at medium or closer is re-run on soul_2 with that character's Soul.
About the pictures. Each tile tries to load the anchor that was actually generated for it. Those files live on the account's CDN, which a sandboxed page is not allowed to reach — so in the published view you will see the frame's identity card instead, and the real image appears when this file is opened from your own copy of the repository. The frame id, role, handles and job id under every tile are correct either way, and they are what you actually need at the canvas.

Four ways to order the same 23 shots

The cut as built peaks at the vortex, two minutes and fifty seconds in, and then runs two minutes of falling action — and Oriane dies at 3:18, after which two strangers arrive cold. Both are real weaknesses. Folding Movement II's back half into Movement III's front half is the obvious fix, so here is what each version of that actually costs.

Why option B and option C are not the same argument. The full cross-cut was already run against the current structure and lost five criteria to three — and its two fatal losses were source fidelity and one-viewing legibility, both of which come from the same thing: alternation reads as simultaneity, and Chapter III opens “The next day…”. A single establishing beat placed early is not alternation. It buys the emotional continuity without asking the audience to infer a causal link the source denies. That is the whole reason B is cheap and C is not.
