Source backcheck

All 26 asset prompts and all 23 shot prompts were read line by line against the novel. Most of it holds — the left calf, the lightning between her teeth, the dark cloud that does not move, the children among the Keepers, equal in height for two boys eighteen months apart. Eight things did not, and they are listed below with what was done about each. Three of them were errors; three were gaps; two are deliberate departures that should stay departures.

What the novel saysWhat the prompts saidResolution
“Caedom shivered.” Ch I, immediately before Oriane is introduced. S2 gave the shiver to Oriane — “She shivers once.” Fixed. The attribution is now back on Caedom, and it is worth more than a correction. The immortal who sees all of time is the one who flinches; the woman about to be told her world ends is not. Handing that beat to the frightened character makes it ordinary. S2 now says so explicitly so the model cannot quietly hand it back.
The Keepers kneel after the shot halts and the sand converges — “as if to finally acknowledge the destruction before them.” S19 had them kneel on arrival; S20 then began the suspension. The order was inverted. Fixed. The inversion made the kneel look like the trigger — power summoned by a gesture. In the novel it is the opposite: the miracle is already underway and they bow to avoid watching it. The kneel moved into S20, after the suspension, and S19 now ends with them standing.
“Oriane stood encased in crystal… it was this crystal that elevated her physical body”; later “her crystalized body”, and lightning that “cut through her hollow and crystal” at the cheek. Every prompt treated it as a shell she wears, ending at the throat — and after the filter pass, as a “fitted protective garment”. Fixed, and it fixes the filter problem too. The crystal is a state of her body, not clothing — which is why it reaches her cheek. Rewritten as a transformation: transparent lattice over the face so the features still read for identity training, opaque faceted plate below the collarbone. A body made of mineral raises no question about what it is or is not wearing, so the wording that keeps the classifier quiet is also the wording the novel actually supports. The garment framing is gone.
“Both held bodies garbed in robes of light” — both of them, in the opening paragraph. Only Caedom had a robe. Oriane had none in any prompt. Fixed. She now wears one over the crystal, in the sheet and in both plates. It costs nothing and it restores the visual rhyme the scene is built on: two beings dressed identically, one of them complete.
The reef “dropped off into the abyss”; the planet “the third rock from the Sun”; “bits of iron ore, taken from stone by those who understood metals”. BG-bloom, BG-machira and PROP-ore were listed in the folder tree with no prompt behind them — and each is required by a shot that exists (S16, S13, S11). Written. Three new assets, three variants each. The folder tree also promised FX-hail, FX-suspend, FX-blast and LEV-fins: suspend and blast are stages inside existing sequences, the inflating fins are now the B variant of the leviathan, and hail is used by no shot — so the tree was corrected rather than four more prompts invented to justify it.
“countless fins, all which now spanned outwards and filled with air, puffing up like leather balloons” — this is what holds the creature in the sky. The creature sheet said only “dozens of fins run in rows down both flanks”. The inflation, which is the whole idea, was nowhere. Written. A four-stage motion study — folded, spanning, filling, inflated — as LEV-body variant B. It also explains why she can stand on one, which the shot list already asks her to do.
“They stripped to their shorts and dove into the tide.” The brothers' sheet has them fully clothed in tunics and trousers, with no bare torsos in the negative tail. Departure, kept. This is the one place the film should not follow the novel. Photoreal eight- and six-year-olds with skin-texture language across a bare torso is not a prompt worth writing whatever any filter permits. The swim is now played entirely as backlit silhouette — a third variant exists for exactly this — which solves it by physics rather than by wardrobe, and looks better than the literal version would have.
Lightning “summoning blood from her veins”; “She cried and bled”; heads that separate, explode and implode. The damage sheet renders all of it as dry fractured mineral. No blood anywhere. Departure, kept. Made under filter pressure, but it earns its place: cracked crystal reads as damage to something that was never supposed to break, which is more unsettling than a wound. The novel's gore is a prose effect and does not survive translation to a held photoreal frame anyway.
Two inventions worth knowing you made. The novel says “one brother blonde and the other brunette” and never says which is which — the sheet assigns dark to Alder and blonde to Wren, and that is a choice, not a reading. And the Founding Stone is never given a colour anywhere in these three chapters; green-white is ours. Both are fine to keep. Both should be locked deliberately rather than discovered later when a frame contradicts one.
Phase 0 — before a single film frame

Asset prompts

Twenty-nine assets, four prompts each — 116 in total. Every asset carries the line from the novel it answers to, and four ways to make it. A the continuity pass on a plain cyc: the one that trains the Soul and locks the face. B the same subject built in its own location under its own lighting plate, which is what seeds the anchor frames. C a deliberately different medium — film stock, engraving, matte painting, lab photography — to break the default digital look. D the chroma pass: the same subject with colour and contrast pushed to the ceiling.

These are not shots. Generate the A pass of all twenty-nine first, on plain backgrounds, and the faces stop drifting for the rest of the production. Everything after that is optional.

Read this before you generate a single D. The film's colour design is built on restraint — Movement II is specified as fully desaturated precisely so that the gold of the Sun and the crimson of the island land against it. The D lane deliberately breaks that rule. It exists for the things that sit outside the film: the poster, the title sequence, the end card, the sixty-second social cut, and the thumbnail that has to survive being two centimetres wide in a festival grid. Pull a D frame into the film itself and you flatten the contrast the whole structure depends on. Generate them, keep them in 99 · LOOKDEV, and use them where colour is competing for attention rather than telling a story.
How the fight escalates without a line of dialogue

Power states — two ladders, one ceiling

Movement II is a hundred and eighteen seconds of continuous escalation, and escalation only works if the audience can see the rung. The novel hands the whole system over in one line, spoken by Oriane about a wielder who understands hollows as well as she does:

Chapter II “He may understand, but he does not have the Stone itself. He lacks the core, the crux of it, the emanating essence. The fullness of it is mine alone.” That is a hard ceiling and a visual tell in the same sentence. Understanding climbs to six. The seventh is the core, and only a Stone-bearer has one. So an adept's aura is bright at the rim and black at the centre; a bearer's is brightest at the centre and falls off outward with no edge at all. The hierarchy of the entire film is legible in a single frame, and nobody has to explain it.
Ladder A · attunement

What the Turned climb

A1–A6. Understanding of one element — water and its phases, energy, gravity, metals, hollows, dominion. It is earned, it is visible as aura structure, and it stops at six forever. Every pursuer in the film sits on this ladder, including the Threadwright at its top.

A7 does not exist for them. That is the point of the chase: sixteen of them, escalating, and not one can reach what she already holds.

Ladder M · the mind

What Oriane climbs

She is A7 in the first frame and never climbs it — she has the Stone before the film starts. Putting her on the attunement ladder would be the obvious mistake and it would flatten the movement, because a character who begins at the ceiling has nowhere to go.

So her escalation is a second, orthogonal axis: how many pieces her mind is split into. M1 → M2 → M10 → M16 → M32 → M64 → M128 → M200+, exactly as the novel counts it. Every rung buys present-tense power and spends foresight, so the ladder is a cost curve, not a power-up.

The schedule — where each rung lands

The two ladders are interleaved on purpose. Every time she takes a rung, they answer with one, so the gap never closes and the tension never resolves early. The one exception is S9, where the film leaves her entirely to show the Threadwright at A6 — hollow-centred — which is the audience's first sight of the ceiling.

The trap to avoid. A ladder that only brightens reads as a power-up, and then her death lands as a victory lap rather than a cost. Every rung above M16 must visibly take something — a cracked pauldron, a burnt-out patch of sheen, an outline going soft at the extremities, a wing-aura guttering on one side. The B variant of the mind ladder exists solely to keep that counter-evidence in front of you while you generate.

Modelling it — states are modifiers, not assets

Eight states across four characters at four variants each would be a hundred and twenty-eight sheets, and it would be waste: the identity does not change between rungs, only the aura does. So generate identity once and carry state as a text fragment appended to any prompt. Three ladder sheets lock the progression visually; everything after that is injection.

Inside the submission project

Folder structure

Two rules drive this. Everything used in the film has to stay in the project, because that is how the generation history gets verified — so nothing is ever deleted, only parked. And the pipeline is image-first, so the folders follow production stage, not scene number: the 39 anchors are made as one string in film order, and shots chain across scene boundaries.

When it comes out wrong

Tweaks & export

Symptom on the left, the one dial that fixes it on the right. Try the dial before you re-roll — a re-roll on a prompt problem just buys the same failure twice.

SymptomThe dial
Face changed between two neighbouring shotsThe start frame is doing all the identity work. Switch that shot to a model with true image references — seedance_2_0, wan3_0, minimax_h3 — and feed the Soul reference alongside the frame. Do not fix this by re-rolling.
Camera moves when you said locked-offPut the movement instruction first and last in the prompt, in plain words: “Static locked-off shot, no camera movement at any point…” then close with “The camera does not move.” On wan3_0 turn on enable_thinking. Affects S4 S15 S17 S19.
Motion is frantic / too much happensLower cfg_scale and raise the duration. A 14s shot with one event reads as confidence; a 6s shot with four events reads as a trailer. Add the word unhurried — it works on every model here.
The chain seam is visibleThe shared anchor is not doing its job. A hidden cut needs genuine near-black or genuine blinding glare — 70% obscured reads as a failed cut, which is worse than an honest one. Rebuild B01 / B02 darker, not the video.
Shimmer / crawling texture on long shotsDeflicker before upscaling, always. Extended and chained generations shimmer at the join; upscaling first bakes it in permanently and there is no route back.
Model refuses the 21:9 frameNot every model does scope. gemini_omni is 16:9 / 9:16 only. Generate at the model's native ratio and use reframe in finishing rather than fighting the generator.
Shot has failed three timesStop. It leaves the queue and goes to the escalation ladder in Revision rules — it does not get a quiet fourth attempt, and it does not block the shots behind it.
Credits vanishing faster than expectedSmart duration (-1) on wan3_0 bills as 10s whatever it returns. Set durations explicitly. Do look-development on gemini_omni and the unlimited-trial models, which cost nothing.
Delivery

Exporting, in the only order that works

1 · DeflickerEvery extended or chained shot, individually, before anything else touches it.
2 · UpscaleTo 2K/4K with the aigc preset. Bring the 720p Seedance 2.5 material (S2) up here or it will sit visibly soft beside its 4K neighbours.
3 · GradeThree looks, one per movement: gold-on-black, cold blue-grey with no warmth anywhere, then golden hour. The grade is what makes 23 separate generations read as one film.
4 · AssembleCinema Studio, in shot order, at 2.39:1. Cut each shot to the exact duration in the shot list — the timing was already proved on the stills reel at Gate C.
5 · SoundScore everything except S13. That shot is 15 seconds of orbital silence and every instinct you have will be to fill it. Do not.
6 · MasterExport the full 4:53 at the festival's stated spec. Keep the per-shot masters — if one shot gets a note, you re-export one shot, not the film.
7 · SubmitThrough the Cinema Studio festival project, with a full day of margin. Re-read all 23 shot prompts and all 39 composed anchor prompts first as a stranger would: they are published after the deadline. Confirm the rules page again — this festival's timeline has already moved once.
When the generator refuses

Why a prompt gets filtered

A safety classifier reads your words literally and without context. It does not know the crystal shell is a costume, or that the leviathan is mythology. Almost every refusal on this film comes from one of the patterns below, and each has a rewrite that keeps the image and loses the trigger.

What trips itThe rewrite
Saying what a covering is notThe original Oriane prompt read “encased from the collarbone down … rather than armour or clothing” with “refractive index that bends whatever sits behind it”. That is a classifier reading adult woman, full body, not clothed, see-through. Never disclaim clothing. Say wearing, say garment, say opaque across the body, and put the refraction at the silhouette edge instead of over the skin.
“Bare” plus a body partBare feet are fine on their own. Bare stacked against a full-length figure and skin-texture language is not. Keep one, drop the rest — state coverage positively (“fully covered, no skin visible below the jaw”) so the model has something to satisfy.
Skin-texture realism on a whole bodyThe anti-retouch module is written for faces. Applied to a full figure it reads as an instruction to render skin, everywhere. Scope it: “natural unretouched face”, not “visible pores” across a standing nude-adjacent body.
Children plus anythingThe brothers' sheet asked for photoreal minors, no shirt, and skin texture across the shoulders. That one the filter was right about, and it is now fully clothed — tunics and trousers, face texture only, with no bare torsos in the negative tail. Keep it that way even where a filter would let it through.
Wounds described as woundsBlood, punctures, torn flesh and weeping fluid read as gore whatever the story reason. Move the damage onto the material: a starred fracture in crystal, a rosette of shattered facets, staining in the plating. Add no blood, no wounds, no gore to the tail. The image survives; the refusal does not.
Body horror verbsPierced, fused, sloughing, split, burned-open, socket are all flags. Say set into, overgrown, waterlogged, scorch-mark, hollow. The uncanny effect comes from stillness and wrongness, not from injury detail — and it reads better for it.
If it still refusesCut the prompt in half and generate each half; the offending clause reveals itself immediately. Do not keep re-rolling a refusal — a filter is deterministic in a way a bad seed is not.

Because the entry goes public

Rights & attribution

The film adapts Chapters I–III of Stone of Matter by B.L. Barkey, who is also the director. There is no third party to hide from — but there is a plan to protect, because 36 more chapters are coming. This section reverses advice this document gave earlier. It previously said not to rename anything. That was right about ownership and wrong about exposure, and the difference matters enough to say plainly.

Why the earlier advice changedOwnership was never the risk. You own the novel from the moment you wrote it, and no contest entry transfers that. The risk is exposure of the vocabulary: the entry publishes your prompts and generation history, so every coined term you typed — the names, the islands, the Stones, the title — enters a public, machine-readable corpus on someone else's platform, under whatever licence you accepted, years before the novel is finished. That is not a copyright question. It is a branding and control question, and it is the one worth acting on.
Do scrambles work? (“Egwn”, “Rinot”)No — they are the worst of both options. Names are not protected by copyright at all, so a scramble buys you nothing legally. And it buys you nothing practically either: anyone reading “Egwn and Rinot” sees Gwen and Torin, so the vocabulary is still exposed, while you have also failed to build a clean second brand. Either use the real names deliberately, or use genuinely distinct ones. Half-measures give you neither the link nor the separation.
Does entering complicate finishing the novel?Not if the licence is ordinary. A contest licence normally attaches to the entry — the film — not to the underlying work it was adapted from. A non-exclusive, promotional licence in a five-minute film does not reach your manuscript. The clauses that would complicate it are: the word exclusive, anything worded as an assignment, any reference to “underlying works” or “derivative works”, and any moral-rights waiver. Read for those five things specifically.
“If I don't win, it's all mine anyway”No — and this is the assumption worth correcting. Contest licences almost always attach on submission, not on winning: the wording is typically “by entering, you grant…”. Losing does not release you, and entries are usually published regardless of result. Winning generally adds obligations rather than creating the licence. Assume the grant is live the moment you press submit.
What to put on the end cardCredit yourself; do not hand over the link. “Original screen story by B.L. Barkey” gives you authorship and provenance without naming the novel and inviting the licence to be read against it. Only name Stone of Matter if the rules require you to disclose underlying IP — and if they ask, disclose honestly. Never conceal something the entry form actually asks for.
RegistrationIf you are in the US, register the manuscript with the Copyright Office before any of this goes public. Statutory damages and legal fees are only available when registration precedes the infringement. It is cheap, and it is the step people skip.
AI output and authorshipUS copyright requires human authorship, so the raw generated frames may not themselves be protectable. Your novel, your shot design, and the selection and arrangement that make up the film are. The story is protected; the individual pixels may not be.
Third-party IP inside the promptsHandled. Every character prompt closes with original character not resembling any real person or existing property, and no prompt names a living artist, a studio, a game or a brand — including the armour reference behind the current design, which is described in original terms rather than named. Keep it that way: a prompt reading “in the style of [property]” is the one that causes trouble once the generation history is public.
Before anything else: this repository is public. source/chapters-1-3.txt — 7,830 words of the unpublished manuscript — is readable by anyone, and the root LICENSE is Apache 2.0, which grants the world a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free licence to reproduce the work and prepare derivative works of it. That licence was meant for the tooling. Read against the whole repository it reaches your novel, and it is a far larger giveaway than any contest clause. A NOTICE file has been added carving the narrative content out of the Apache grant, which is the strongest same-day mitigation available — but the real fix is to make the repository private and take the manuscript out of it. That is your call to make, not mine, so it has not been done.

The direction

Three chapters, three camera grammars, one contract

You asked to avoid a collection of small clips cut together like a long advertisement. The fix is not "use longer clips" — it is to give each movement a rule the camera obeys, and to make the rules different enough that the audience feels the change even if they never name it.

Movement I — The Sun

Sustained oner

The first 34 seconds contain zero cuts. The camera rises out of fire and drifts through a courtyard of worlds before it finds the two figures. This is the contract with the audience: this film breathes. Everything after it is read against this opening.

Five shots for eighty seconds. Dialogue plays in wide. One 14-second unbroken hold on a face — no coverage, no reverse, no safety.

Movement II — The Ocean

Travelling, not cutting

Action does not require fast cutting; it requires continuous motion. The camera flies with Oriane at her speed, so the world blurs while she stays stable in frame.

Cuts here are earned by perspective reassignment — we leave her only twice, and both times to occupy someone else's eyes. Inside a shot, speed ramps do the work a cut would normally do.

Movement III — The Island

Locked-off witness

The boys are children watching something they don't understand. So the camera stops moving entirely. Twelve ships bombard a temple inside a static frame — far more frightening than a shaking one.

The miracle is one unbroken 14-second wide. Then, at the very end, the only push-in in the movement, and it lands on the thesis of the film.

Shot economyRule 01
Budget generations, not seconds. Twenty-three is the number. Every time you want to add a shot, extend an existing one instead and ask what the cut was actually buying you.
Motivated cuts onlyRule 02
A cut is permitted for exactly three reasons: a new point of view, a jump in time or place, or a revelation the current frame cannot hold. "The clip ran out" is not one of them — that is what extension and frame-chaining are for.
Hide the seams the story gives youRule 03
The source text hands over its own wipes. Movement II: the wall of water, the translucent warping void-bubbles, the leviathan's flank filling frame, disintegrating bodies, the black centre of the vortex. Movement III: "a great dust storm stirred up, obscuring all shapes into dark-brown shadows" — that is a written-in cut point — plus the "bright hollow" of the Temple doors and the mists rushing the brothers. Chain your clips so the seam always lands inside one of these.
Ramp instead of cutRule 04
Cinema Studio v2 exposes speedramp with slowmo, speedup and impact. A ramp inside a single generation delivers the same accent a cut would — without breaking the world, the light, or the face.
Cut inside the generationRule 05
When you genuinely need coverage — the bombardment, the Keepers emerging — use multi_shots with multi_prompt. The cuts happen inside one generation, so lighting, costume, geography and faces stay locked across them. Editorial cuts between separate generations are where continuity dies.
Silence at the climaxRule 06
The vortex resolves with no sound, in wide, from orbit. Killing the audio at the biggest moment is the single strongest tool available and it costs nothing. Everyone else's climax will be loud.
Hold two beats past comfortableRule 07
After Oriane disintegrates, stay on the empty frame. After the mountain falls, stay on the flat ocean. The held empty frame is the most reliable signal of authorship in the entire medium, and it is one of the few things a model cannot accidentally produce.
Structure

Four ways to blend the three chapters

The document gives you a conversation, a battle, and a childhood memory — in that order, separated by 108 years and one day. Straight chronology is the weakest of the four options, because your protagonist dies at minute three and the film then has to introduce two strangers cold.

Option A

Linear relay

Chapter I, then II, then III, as written, with time cards between. Most faithful, simplest to produce, easiest to sequence.

The problem: the emotional baton hits the floor at 3:18. Oriane dies and the film restarts with two characters we have no reason to care about yet.

Option B — recommended

The framed prophecy

Caedom's scene is not a prologue, it is the spine. His voice carries across both other movements. Oriane's death intercuts with him watching from the Sun — and the text explicitly supports this: "She looked up to the Sun where she knew Caedom was watching."

Then the boys' chapter becomes the answer to Ori's opening question. She asked why release him. Caedom's reply — "there must be risk if there is to be growth" — plays over an eight-year-old's face as he decides he wants to raise mountains over his enemies. Close on the frozen tear from minute one, now revealed as the last image.

Option C

The two witnesses

Cross-cut Oriane's last stand against the boys' swim, so her sacrifice and their innocent adventure run in parallel. Her Stone launches "thousands of miles away" exactly as they surface at Keeper's Isle.

The problem: parallel action wants cutting. It fights the long-hold mandate directly. Powerful, but it costs you the thing that makes this film distinct.

Option D — layer onto B

The dark cloud POV

Chapter III contains one throwaway line: "One dark cloud watched them from afar." Make that the connective tissue. The camera becomes the darkness — it finds Oriane's island, it follows her over the ocean, and in the final movement it is simply sitting behind Alder's shoulder, out of focus, while he decides what he wants.

Cheap, reusable, and it converts three separate stories into one continuous act of surveillance. Layer it under Option B rather than choosing between them.

What the text actually says

Three passages decide this, and all three cut against causal interlacing — not on taste, on fact.

Evidence 01 — they are not simultaneous

"And then she disintegrated… never to return to this side of the veil.  CHAPTER III — BROTHERS.  The next day…"

The two events are a day apart, and the film would have to either fabricate simultaneity or run a card that lies. Not fatal on its own — adaptations compress time constantly — but it means the interlace starts by overriding the text rather than serving it.

Evidence 02 — different enemy entirely

"…not mythical sea monsters, but wooden ships with billowing sails. There were twelve of them. Foreign ships approaching rapidly from the west… Alder had heard stories of pirates before that day."

The attackers are mundane human raiders, not the Vesper's wielders. Oriane is fighting void-wielders and a possessed leviathan; the boys are watching a pirate raid. Linking them causally requires inventing a metaphysics the source does not have — and inventing it fast enough that a first-time viewer follows.

Evidence 03 — there was never any jeopardy

"The the Vigil had done it. They had defended their home from an ominous threat. Not only that, but they had done it with ease, remaining shrouded in cloaks and composed with peaceful calm."

This is the one that ends the argument. The outcome was never in doubt — only the boys thought it was. Movement III is a child's-eye experience of an event that was always going to be fine. Interlacing converts a scene about witnessing into a scene about rescue, and there is nothing to rescue.

And the version in the text is bleaker and better than the proposed one. Oriane gives up her Stone, her body and her existence to hide Cairn Island from the Vesper — "leading the darkness away from the Anchor Stone" — and she succeeds. Then the island is attacked anyway, the very next morning, from the west, by something she never knew existed. Her sacrifice bought the island its future, not its next day. "As Ori wins, the attack recedes" is a tidier story and a smaller one.

Favours proposed

The synthesis — take the three wins, pay none of the costs

The instinct behind the proposal is right and the diagnosis is right. The mechanism is the only thing that needs changing — and the text already supplies a better one. Rising action through infection, not through jeopardy. Four changes, +1 generation, no loss of shot economy.

Seed the boys inside Movement IIFixes the baton · +1 shot

How it gets made

Eight phases, five gates

The pipeline is inverted from the obvious one. You do not generate shots and then fix them — you build the film as a string of still anchor frames, approve it while it is still cheap, and only then spend video credits interpolating between images you have already signed off on. Roughly 200 images to 76 video generations.

A gate is a hard stop. Nothing downstream of it begins until it locks. Gates exist because the failure mode on a 24-day schedule is not bad work — it is endless tweaking of work that was already good enough, on day nineteen, while three shots remain ungenerated.

The core asset

The frame string

Every shot has an A-frame and a B-frame. Where two shots join continuously, they share one image — shot N's B-frame is literally shot N+1's A-frame, one file, used twice. That is why 23 shots need 46 slots but only 39 images.

Click any slot for what that frame has to contain. Gold slots are shared joins; dashed indigo slots are bridge frames — authored images of the seam itself; coral slots are matched pairs designed together across a transition.

Why this makes rearranging nearly free

Once the film exists as a string of anchors, structure becomes an editing problem instead of a regeneration problem. Every operation below costs images, not video.

Reorder two shotsCosts: 2 images
Swap them in the ledger. Only the two new joins need frames — everything inside each shot is untouched. The old join frames go in the bin, not the bonfire.
Insert a shotCosts: 3 images
Generate its A and B frames plus one bridge into the neighbour. The shots on either side keep their existing anchors and never regenerate.
Cut a shotCosts: 1 image
Delete it and generate a single bridge frame joining its former neighbours. If those neighbours already shared a look, often even that is unnecessary.
Split a shot that keeps failingCosts: 1 image
Generate one mid-frame and turn a 14s generation into two chained 7s generations against the same outer anchors. Shorter spans are dramatically more reliable — this is the standard rescue, not a compromise.
Change the whole gradeCosts: 0 images
Colour lives in the finish, not the generation. Do not re-roll anchors to chase a look you can reach in the grade — a mistake that has eaten more AI-film schedules than any other.
Promote a discovered frameCosts: 0 images
When a video generation produces something better than the anchor you fed it, extract its final frame and promote it into the ledger as the new B-frame. The film improves by harvesting its own accidents. Do this during the cheap motion pass, before anything is final.
Discipline

Revision rules — when to re-roll and when to stop

Re-rolling is the most expensive habit in AI filmmaking because it feels like progress. These rules exist so a bad shot resolves in hours rather than consuming a week. Three attempts, then you stop changing the dice and start changing the input.

1st
Re-roll unchanged, up to three timesSame anchors, same prompt, same model. Generation is stochastic and the third roll is often the one. If a shot has not landed in three, the dice are not the problem — stop rolling.
2nd
Change the anchor frames, not the promptThree failures means the model cannot get from your A-frame to your B-frame in the time allowed. Usually the two frames are too far apart. Move them closer together, or re-generate the B-frame nearer the A. This fixes more failures than prompt rewriting ever will.
3rd
Split the spanGenerate one mid-frame and run two shorter chained generations against the same outer anchors. A 14-second ask becomes two 7-second asks. The cut is invisible because both halves share a real frame — you have not added a cut, you have added an anchor.
Last
Change what the shot isIf a span fails after splitting, the shot is asking for something the tools cannot do. Re-stage it: a locked-off frame instead of a move, a reaction instead of an event, a body wipe instead of a reveal. Do this before the final generation phase, never during it — by then the schedule has no room for it.
Tweak budget

Two passes, then it ships

Each shot gets one revision pass after its first accepted version, and one after picture lock. A third pass requires cutting something else — time is conserved, not created.

Batch, don't iterate

Four variants beats four attempts

Generate anchor frames in batches of four at once and pick, rather than rolling one at a time and judging in isolation. Choosing between options is fast; evaluating a single image against an imagined ideal is not.

Note discipline

Notes name the frame, not the feeling

"Movement II feels flat" cannot be actioned. "FRAME-14 reads warm; the threadwright's world has no warmth in it" can be, in one image, in four minutes.

Payload

The layered truths, and where each one lands

You asked for multiple truths delivered in layers. These are already in the source — the work is making sure each one has a frame to live in, so the film can be watched five times and give something different each pass.

01

Trust is asked for before the reason is given

"Leave the 'whys' to me, child. That is trust. If you trust me first, the answers will come after."

Carried by: S5, in an unbroken hold with his hands on her face. No cutaway, no music swell. If the camera stays put, the line has to survive on its own — which is the same demand the line is making.

02

Risk is the price of growth, and the bill is real

"He will almost surely annihilate everything. But it is the way. Balance. There must be risk if there is to be growth."

Carried by: S3 as dialogue, then repeated in voice-over across S22 — over Alder's face. Heard at minute one it sounds like wisdom. Heard at minute five, over a child discovering he wants power, it sounds like a cost estimate.

03

Godhood is a position on a ladder, not a category

"To a firefly, the eagle is a god… You are the seed who can become the tree. The pebble, the mountain."

Carried by: the pebble-to-mountain image is literalised in S20 — the Keepers raise sand and dead coral into a floating mountain. Caedom's metaphor from minute one becomes a physical object in minute four. Never point at it.

04

The discipline of power is refusal

"It was powerful and effective, yes. But more than this, it was addicting."

Carried by: S12–S13. Oriane's greatest technique is the one she swore never to use, and she is smiling when she breaks the promise — the text is explicit that she enjoys it. She only breaks it for something other than herself, and it still destroys her. Play the joy, not the sacrifice; the sacrifice reads on its own.

05

Knowing the secret spoils nothing

"She remembered wishing as a child that novels would come out and share the secret from the start. She promised to do just this if she ever wrote one."

Carried by: the film tells you its ending early and dares you to still care. This also happens to be an argument about this exact festival — every prompt is published afterwards, the whole method goes open, and the work is still the work. A film whose theme is "revealing the method costs you nothing" entering a contest that publishes your method is not a coincidence you should waste.

06

The same miracle plants two different seeds

"Mum and Dad would be so proud." — "I want to move mountains. To raise them up high over the heads of my enemies."

Carried by: S22, and this is the film. Two brothers watch the identical event from the identical patch of grass. Wren sees belonging. Alder sees dominion. The Unending War is not fought between armies, it is fought in the half-second where a person decides what they just saw meant. Everything preceding this exists so this lands.

07

Darkness enters by invitation

"The outsiders never would've found them if dark hearts had remained absent from her island."

Carried by: the dark cloud of Option D. It is never attacked and never explained. It is simply present in Movement III, out of focus behind a child, having already been let in.

08

Death is a door; erasure is the actual stake

"It would mean not just death, but erasure. The erasing of ever having existed at all."

Carried by: the held empty frame after S14. Oriane is gone and the frame keeps running. The film has already established that nothing truly ends — so the two extra seconds of nothing are not grief, they are a question about whether that was true.
