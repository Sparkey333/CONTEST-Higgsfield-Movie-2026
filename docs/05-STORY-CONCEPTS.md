# 05 — Story Concepts: Six Engineered to Win

Six original concepts, each built against the convergence thesis in
[`01-JUDGES-DOSSIER.md`](01-JUDGES-DOSSIER.md) § 4: **one coherent space, one motivated light
source, long held takes, a face that carries a scene, real weight, a story that turns.**

Every one is designed to be *makeable by one person in 24 days* — 1–2 locations, 1–2 characters,
a light source AI renders well, and spectacle that comes from atmosphere and scale rather than
choreography.

Scored in [`06-WARGAME.md`](06-WARGAME.md). **Add your own to
[`../script/IDEAS-INBOX.md`](../script/IDEAS-INBOX.md) and they get run through the same harness.**

---

## The design constraints every concept here satisfies

| Constraint | Why |
|---|---|
| ≤2 locations | Consistency budget; Anderson's locked-room register |
| ≤2 speaking characters | ~800 generations to lock each character sheet |
| ONE named light source | The Papamichael filter; kills the two-suns tell |
| Spectacle from atmosphere, not choreography | Fire, dust, water, weather generate beautifully; fights don't |
| A turn at ~60% | Catmull's structural requirement |
| An object through-line | Free thematic layering, cheap inserts, always in continuity |
| A recontextualised final image | The strongest closing move in short form |

# A. VANTAGE

> **A fire lookout, alone in a tower, keeps calling positions on the radio as the fire that will
> reach her becomes the only light she has left to see by.**

**Premise.** A wildfire lookout in a glass tower. Her job is to report — coordinates, wind, spread
— to crews she will never meet. The fire turns. Command tells her to evacuate; the road is already
gone. She stays on the radio because her reports are still routing crews away from a valley where
people are sleeping. The tower's electric light fails. She keeps calling positions by the light of
the thing that is killing her.

**The three truths.**
1. *Surface:* a woman refuses to abandon her post.
2. *Beneath:* being useful is not the same as being saved, and she chooses useful.
3. *Underneath that:* the fire is the only reason she can see her own maps. **What destroys you
   can be what illuminates you** — stated purely in light, never in dialogue.

**Signature image.** The glass tower at night, a tiny lit figure inside, the entire horizon behind
her a moving orange line. Held for thirty seconds. Nothing cuts.

**Light logic.** Act I: a single tungsten desk lamp, warm, motivated, hard-edged, everything
beyond the glass black. Act III: the lamp is dead and **the fire is the key light** — flickering,
directional, orange, coming from one side of her face, the other side falling to nothing.
**The film's entire lighting change is a plot event.** Papamichael will notice this in the first
ten seconds of Act III and he will know it was a decision.

**Object through-line.** The radio handset. First: routine, held loosely. Middle: gripped white.
Last: still keyed open on the desk, the room empty, someone's voice still answering.

**Judge fit.**
- **Catmull** — a clean want, a clear turn, a cost paid, and an ending that reframes the opening.
- **Papamichael** — a single motivated source that *changes diegetically*; a face carrying long
  takes; firelight is his native register.
- **Anderson** — a lone woman in a glass box against an apocalyptic scale event. This is almost
  precisely his compositional signature: tiny figure, vast foreboding space, held wide.

**Production.** ★★★★★ — One location. One character. One voice on the radio (no second character
sheet). Fire, smoke and ember-drift are the single best-looking things AI video generates, and
they provide constant atmospheric motion so long holds never look frozen. Slow motion on ember
fall is free spectacle.

**Risk.** Fire scale can drift between takes — lock it with a scale law and a horizon landmark in
the GEO block. Glass reflections are a known failure mode: stage the camera *inside* the tower
looking out, never at the glass at an angle.

# B. TENANT

> **The last resident of a condemned tower block refuses to leave, and the building comes down
> around her one floor at a time.**

**Premise.** An old woman in an apartment she has lived in for fifty years. Demolition has already
started above and beside her. Dust falls from the ceiling into her tea. She makes the bed. She
waters a plant. Machines work closer. She is not protesting; she is simply not leaving. In the
final movement, she sits in the one chair by the one window as the light changes — because the
building next door, which has blocked her window for forty years, is gone, and for the first time
her room is full of sun.

**The three truths.**
1. *Surface:* an eviction resisted.
2. *Beneath:* a life is a room, and dismantling the room dismantles the life.
3. *Underneath that:* the destruction gives her something she never had — light. **Progress is
   not a lie; it is just never free.** The film refuses to take a side, which is what makes it
   land.

**Signature image.** A perfectly made bed. Dust falling through a shaft of light onto it. The wall
behind it visibly shaking.

**Light logic.** One window, one direction, hard shaft, heavy particulate. Then — one cut before
the end — the same window, blown open with light. Same source. New world.

**Object through-line.** A drinking glass on the table. It rings and trembles with every distant
impact. It is the film's seismograph, its clock, and its final image.

**Judge fit.**
- **Catmull** — enormously emotional without sentimentality, and thematically ambivalent in a way
  a Pixar veteran will respect. This is the "...okay, that one is real" film.
- **Papamichael** — one window, hard shaft, dust as a visible light-carrier, an old face in
  available light. This is *Nebraska* energy. He will love it.
- **Anderson** — gets genuine large-scale destruction and a locked room. Slightly lower than
  Vantage on scale, but the collapsing-space geography is very much his.

**Production.** ★★★★★ — One room. One character. Dust, light shafts and falling debris are AI's
strengths. Destruction happens *off-screen and to the walls* — no choreography required.

**Risk.** An elderly face is a harder character-sheet lock (texture, asymmetry) — budget extra.
Avoid any shot of machinery operating; keep the demolition an off-screen force.

# C. SALVAGE

> **A deep-sea welder is sent to cut open a wreck and recover what's inside, and recognises the
> ship.**

**Premise.** Pitch black water. A helmet lamp. A woman welding through a hull at depth, talking to
a surface tender through a comm line. She's done this a hundred times. Through the cut, her lamp
finds the inside of a cabin — and a detail she knows: a mark she scratched into a bulkhead as a
child, on her father's boat, the one that went down with him. The recovery job is a body. The job
does not change. She finishes it.

**The three truths.**
1. *Surface:* a salvage job.
2. *Beneath:* grief is work you have already been trained to do.
3. *Underneath that:* she never tells the surface. The audience holds a secret with her that no
   one in the film knows. **Withheld truth is the most intimate kind.**

**Signature image.** A cone of lamplight in absolute black, finding a child's scratched initials
in steel.

**Light logic.** The helmet lamp is the **only** light in the film. Everything outside its cone is
true black. This is the most forgiving light design imaginable for AI generation — the model
cannot make a two-suns error in a frame that is 90% black — and simultaneously the most dramatic.

**Object through-line.** The cutting torch. Its light is both the tool and the threat.

**Judge fit.**
- **Catmull** — a devastating, contained emotional turn.
- **Papamichael** — a single practical source, total commitment, faces in hard raking light.
- **Anderson** — *Event Horizon* in a diving suit. Vast dark, locked space, a small figure,
  dread. He will feel spoken to directly.

**Production.** ★★★★☆ — Extreme darkness is a massive consistency advantage. Underwater
particulate gives free atmospheric motion. The helmet obscures the face for much of the runtime,
which lowers identity-drift risk enormously.

**Risk.** The helmet is a double-edged sword — it protects consistency but **hides the face**, and
your strongest asset with Catmull and Papamichael is a face. Solve it deliberately: build in a
reason for the visor to show her face (interior helmet glow from the display, reflected torchlight)
for the two shots that matter most. Underwater physics/buoyancy needs explicit prompting.

# D. SECOND LANGUAGE

> **A court interpreter is the only person in the room who understands both sides, and she has
> four seconds to decide whether to translate what was actually said.**

**Premise.** A hearing room. A witness testifies in a language only the interpreter speaks. He
says something that will destroy his own case — a true thing, said badly. She renders it. Then he
says something else, and we watch her calculate. The camera stays on her while he speaks. The
film is a single face doing arithmetic with someone else's life.

**The three truths.**
1. *Surface:* a procedural moment in a hearing.
2. *Beneath:* every act of translation is an act of authorship.
3. *Underneath that:* **the audience is put in her position** — subtitles give us the truth, the
   room gets her version, and we are made complicit in the gap. The counterpoint device isn't a
   flourish here; it's the entire architecture.

**Signature image.** A close-up held for twenty-two seconds while a voice speaks off-screen, and
nothing happens except a decision.

**Light logic.** High institutional windows, one direction, cold, unflattering, no fill. Utterly
motivated, utterly unglamorous.

**Object through-line.** Her notepad. Shorthand she stops taking.

**Judge fit.**
- **Catmull** — a pure moral turn, a genuinely original structure, and it does something only
  cinema can do.
- **Papamichael** — **he shot *The Trial of the Chicago 7*** — the courtroom film Higgsfield's
  own page leads with — and has spoken about the challenge of making a static legal setting
  visually alive. This concept is aimed directly at the thing he is proudest of solving.
- **Anderson** — **the weak point.** No spectacle, no scale, no space. He will respect the craft
  and be bored.

**Production.** ★★★★★ — Cheapest concept here. One room, effectively one performance, two faces.
The 22-second held close-up is *exactly* what AI does well and what nobody else will attempt.

**Risk.** Anderson's score caps this. Also the highest dialogue burden of the six — and dialogue
is where AI generation is weakest (lip-sync, invented mumble). Mitigate by keeping the *testimony*
off-screen and the interpreter mostly silent: the face is the film.

# E. THE LISTENING

> **In the rubble of a collapsed building, a rescue worker calls for silence and puts her ear to
> the concrete — and something answers.**

**Premise.** Night, a debris field, work lights. The protocol is: everything stops, everyone
listens, sixty seconds. She hears knocking. The team digs toward it for the length of the film.
The knocking gets stronger, then weaker, then stops. They break through to find the source: a
water pipe, ticking as it cools. And in the silence after that failure, from somewhere else
entirely, another knock.

**The three truths.**
1. *Surface:* a rescue.
2. *Beneath:* hope is a discipline you practise, not a feeling you have.
3. *Underneath that:* the false hope did not waste the night — **it kept them digging long enough
   to be there for the real one.** The film argues that being wrong is load-bearing.

**Signature image.** Twenty people frozen absolutely still in a floodlit debris field, all of them
listening. Held. No cut.

**Light logic.** Portable work lights on stands — hard, directional, low, throwing enormous
shadows up across the rubble. One source per setup, always visible in frame or clearly implied.

**Object through-line.** A piece of rebar used to knock back. Three knocks. Answer.

**Judge fit.**
- **Catmull** — a genuinely sophisticated thematic argument, and a huge emotional payload.
- **Papamichael** — hard practical work lights in darkness, real faces, real dust, no glamour.
- **Anderson** — a vast, textured, hostile space with small figures in it, and rubble geography
  is very much his idiom.

**Production.** ★★★☆☆ — The most expensive here. The "twenty people frozen" shot is a **crowd
shot**, which is a known high-risk failure mode (count drift). Solvable — one crowd asset with a
height/clothing range, an explicit count, and most figures in silhouette — but it costs.

**Risk.** Crowd management, plus a debris-field location sheet is harder to lock than a room.
Consider reducing the team to **three people** — it costs almost nothing dramatically and removes
the single biggest technical risk in the concept.

# F. THE GHOST LIGHT

> **An old actor rehearses, alone in a dark theatre, for a part he has already been replaced in.**

**Premise.** A bare stage, one bare bulb on a stand — the *ghost light*, the real theatrical
tradition of leaving a single light burning in an empty house. An aging actor runs his lines to
two thousand empty seats. He is very good. We gradually understand he is not preparing — he is the
understudy, he was never going to go on, and tonight was the closing performance he wasn't called
for. He finishes the speech anyway, to nobody. Then he turns the ghost light off, which is the one
thing you are never supposed to do.

**The three truths.**
1. *Surface:* an actor rehearses.
2. *Beneath:* work done for no audience is still work.
3. *Underneath that:* the film is about **being replaced** — and it is showing at a festival
   judged by a man taking public criticism for legitimising the technology that replaces people.
   It never says the word "AI." It doesn't have to.

**Signature image.** One bare bulb, a man, and two thousand empty seats receding into black.

**Light logic.** The single most elegant in this document. **One bare practical bulb, in frame,
for the entire film.** Every shadow radiates from a source the audience can see. Zero ambiguity,
zero possibility of a light-logic error, and unimprovably motivated.

**Object through-line.** The ghost light itself.

**Judge fit.**
- **Catmull** — devastating, and *about the thing he is being criticised for*. High risk, high
  reward: he may find it the most moving film in the competition, or he may find it manipulative.
- **Papamichael** — the cleanest light design of the six. A face, a bulb, and darkness.
- **Anderson** — a vast dark space and a tiny figure. Better than Second Language, weaker than
  Vantage or Salvage.

**Production.** ★★★★★ — One location, one character, one light. The cheapest film here to keep
consistent, and the auditorium's darkness hides everything.

**Risk.** **The meta-commentary is the risk.** A jury that reads it as flattery, or as an
anti-AI film winning an AI contest, could bounce hard. It is the highest-variance concept in this
document — genuinely capable of first place or of missing the shortlist. Also: it is the concept
most likely to be *independently invented* by other entrants, because the ghost light is a
well-known image.

# HYBRIDS WORTH CONSIDERING

The concepts are modular. Two combinations are stronger than their parts:

- **Vantage + Second Language** — the lookout's radio reports *are* translation: she decides what
  to report, and people live or die on her edit. Adds Second Language's moral architecture to
  Vantage's spectacle, fixing the Anderson gap. **This may be the strongest single idea in the
  document.**
- **Tenant + Ghost Light** — the last tenant is a retired performer, and the room is her stage.
  Adds Ghost Light's thematic depth to Tenant's spectacle without the meta-risk.
