# Genre Strategy — Which Lanes Actually Win

*What to make, what to avoid, and why.*

---

## The Method

Three filters, applied in order. A genre has to survive all three.

1. **Does the medium do it well?** AI video has hard, known strengths and hard, known failures. Genres whose native subject matter sits on the strength side get a free 20% on Technical Execution before you write a word.
2. **Is the lane crowded?** With unlimited entries and a $1M pool, some lanes will have hundreds of submissions. A juror on entry #300 of the same thing is not a juror you can win.
3. **Does it satisfy all three judges?** Catmull wants an arc, Papamichael wants motivated light, Anderson wants a hook. Most genres serve one and starve two.

### The capability map — memorize this

| AI video is **good** at | AI video is **bad** at |
|---|---|
| Landscape, weather, scale | Two humans interacting physically |
| **Water, fire, smoke, dust, ice** | Hands doing precise tasks |
| Texture, decay, materials, rust | Faces sustained across many shots |
| Animals and non-human subjects | Dialogue and lip sync |
| Slow single-subject motion | Continuity of complex geography |
| Static, locked-off compositions | Crowds with individuality |
| Atmosphere and light through media | Object permanence across cuts |
| Machines, vehicles, architecture | Anything needing exact repetition |

**Every genre recommendation below is derived from that table.** The winning move is not to fight the medium's weaknesses with better prompting — it is to pick a story whose subject matter never asks for them.

## The Flood — What Everyone Else Is Making

Predicted saturation, highest first. **These are the lanes to stay out of.**

| Lane | Why it floods | Why it loses |
|---|---|---|
| **Cyberpunk neon-rain city** | The single most-generated AI aesthetic in existence | Papamichael will see 200 of these. Unmotivated neon fill is the exact thing he punishes. |
| **Fake blockbuster trailers** | Easiest thing to make that *looks* impressive | Dies with all three. No arc, no ending, no film. |
| **Post-apocalyptic wasteland** | Default prompt output | No story engine. Atmosphere isn't a plot. |
| **Fantasy epic with armies** | Everyone wants their *Hell Grind* | Crowds are AI's worst failure mode. You cannot win the arms race Higgsfield already won with $500K. |
| **Space opera** | Genre default | Hard to make emotionally specific in 4 minutes. |
| **Abstract / surreal "AI art film"** | Cheap to make, hides flaws | Anderson has nothing to hold onto. Reads as an inability to write. |
| **Mecha / kaiju** | Great spectacle | Mech + crowd + human face = three hard problems in one film. |

**One nuance worth naming:** Higgsfield's own showcase films — *Hell Grind*, *Zephyr*, *Mork* — will anchor everyone's expectation of what a "good Higgsfield film" looks like, and hundreds of entrants will imitate them. Studying the *pipeline* is smart. Copying the *genre* puts you in direct comparison with a $500,000 production. Don't.

## The Tier List

### TIER S — highest expected value

**1 · Elegiac landscape ("place as character")**
A person alone in a vast, changed environment. One figure, one location, weather and water doing the heavy lifting.

- ✅ **Medium:** hits nearly every strength — landscape, water, scale, texture, single-subject motion, atmosphere.
- ✅ **Crowd:** genuinely uncrowded, because it isn't "cool" and it doesn't demo well in a prompt.
- ✅ **Judges:** Papamichael's entire filmography. Catmull gets a simple human arc. Anderson gets his set piece if the landscape *does* something — floods, collapses, burns, freezes.
- **Sub-niches:** drowned towns and reservoir drawdowns · abandoned infrastructure with a caretaker · drought · glacier and ice · a mine · burn scar · the last working day of a working landscape.
- 🎯 **This is SILT.**

**2 · Wordless animal POV**
An animal is the protagonist. No dialogue, no lip sync, no language.

- ✅ **Medium:** animals are an AI strength and the two hardest problems — faces and dialogue — are eliminated by design.
- ✅ **Crowd:** rare, because it's hard to write and doesn't look impressive in a still.
- ✅ **Judges:** Catmull's whole tradition. And it is by an enormous margin **the best Audience Choice play available** — it crosses every language barrier on earth.
- **Sub-niches:** a dog waiting · a working animal at the end of its usefulness · migration · an animal in a human space that's being dismantled.
- 🎯 **This is THE DOG AT THE DOOR.**

**3 · Single-location pressure piece**
One room, one or two people, a clock running.

- ✅ **Medium:** the consistency surface is tiny. One lighting geometry. Locked-off camera.
- ✅ **Crowd:** uncrowded, because it requires actual writing.
- ⚠️ **Judges:** Anderson needs a genre engine — a countdown, a threat, a reversal. Give him one.

---

### TIER A — strong, with a caveat each

**4 · Industrial / procedural craft**
Someone performing skilled physical work, filmed with respect for the process.

- The most **under-exploited** lane in AI film, and the one with the highest **authenticity moat**: it only works if the maker actually knows the trade, and almost nobody does. **This is where a P.E. has an unfair advantage.** Weld, cast, survey, dredge, drill, rig, splice.
- ⚠️ Needs a dramatic engine layered on top, or it's a process video.

**5 · Folk horror / rural dread**
Landscape-driven horror. Fog, treeline, isolation, wrongness.

- ✅ AI is superb at fog, forest, and ambiguity — and horror *benefits* from the uncanny artifacts that hurt other genres.
- ✅ Anderson made *Event Horizon*. This is his register.
- ⚠️ Less crowded than creature horror, but not empty. Win on restraint.

**6 · Near-future domestic sci-fi**
One speculative idea, one ordinary home, one family. The *Black Mirror* shape.

- ✅ High-concept hook for Anderson, real stakes for Catmull, and domestic interiors are cheap and consistent.
- ⚠️ Usually needs dialogue, which is a real cost. Solve it with one voice and no reverse angles.

**7 · Mythic minimalism**
Myth carried by landscape and a single object rather than by armies.

- ✅ Gets epic scale without crowds. The Creation Stones fit here.
- ⚠️ ⚠️ **Watch the religious-statement disqualifier.** Enoch, Goetia, and Hekhalot material sits right on that line. A myth is fine; an argument about God is a removal.

---

### TIER B — viable if the execution is exceptional

**8 · Anime-styled action** — crowded, but stylization *hides* AI artifacts rather than exposing them, and the tournament-arc shape is a proven short-form structure. Win on craft, not idea.
**9 · Creature horror** — one creature, mostly unseen, is very achievable. Crowded.
**10 · Period drama** — AI is excellent at texture and costume, poor at sustained faces. Shoot it like *Nebraska*.
**11 · Documentary-fiction hybrid** — an interview-free, archival-feeling piece. Very rare, high ceiling, hard to land.

---

### TIER C — avoid

Everything in **The Flood** table above.

## The Sub-Niche Sweet Spots

The intersection of *uncrowded* and *AI-native*. If you make anything else this cycle, make one of these:

| Sub-niche | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Water arriving** | Flood, refill, tide, dam release. AI's single best-rendered phenomenon, and it is inherently a set piece. |
| **Night work with one practical** | A headlamp, a work light, a fire. Forces the one-source discipline that wins Papamichael, and shrinks the consistency surface to whatever the light touches. |
| **Deep water, ice, cave** | Light discipline is physically forced by the environment. The medium's lighting problem solves itself. |
| **Machines that outlive their purpose** | Turbines, pumps, elevators, satellites. No face, infinite pathos, and AI renders machinery beautifully. |
| **Weather as antagonist** | Whiteout, dust storm, flood, thaw. A villain with no continuity requirements. |
| **The last person doing a dying trade** | Instant arc, instant stakes, and the specificity is unfakeable if you have it. |
| **Grief told through landscape** | Catmull's emotional register delivered entirely through AI's strongest capability. |

## Jury vs. Audience — Two Different Games

They are not the same optimization, and you can run one film at each.

| | **Jury prize** ($500K / $200K / $100K) | **Audience Choice** ($100K) |
|---|---|---|
| Wins on | Craft, arc, restraint, specificity | Shareability, immediacy, emotion |
| Length feel | Earns its 4:22 | Feels like 90 seconds |
| Dialogue | Fine, sparingly | **None.** Language is a border. |
| Subject | Human, specific, local | Animal, child, universal |
| Ending | Earned and quiet | Gut-punch, screenshot-able |
| Score | Restrained, silence used as a tool | Restrained until the last minute, then one clean hit |
| Best lanes | Tier S1, A4, A5, A6 | **Tier S2**, then A5 |

## The Principle Underneath All of This

> **The most defensible niche is the intersection of a genre with a real expertise you hold.**

Genre alone is a lane anyone can enter. Genre plus lived knowledge is a lane with one occupant. A jury cannot always articulate why one film feels true and another feels generated, but they can feel it, and it comes from detail that was *known* rather than *prompted*.

For DarkHearts, that intersection is unusually rich:

| Expertise | Genre lane it unlocks |
|---|---|
| **Water resources / dam & river engineering** | Elegiac landscape, industrial craft, weather-as-antagonist |
| **Colorado high country** | Folk horror, elegiac landscape, weather |
| **Published epic fantasy** | Mythic minimalism |
| **Metal / EDM composition** | Any of them — the score is a differentiator in every lane |
| **BJJ** | Single-location pressure piece, anime-styled action |
| **Dogs** | Wordless animal POV |

Six lanes, all Tier S or Tier A, all with a moat. **Pick from this table and nowhere else.**
