# 01 — The Judges: Full Dossier & Predicted Judging Behaviour

Three judges. Three completely different lenses. The film that wins is not the one that maximises
any single lens — it is the one that **survives all three simultaneously**, because a jury of
three converges on the film nobody objects to before it converges on the film someone loves.

This document has four parts:
1. A deep career dossier on each judge, including the last 12 months
2. What each one is *actually* looking at when they watch your film
3. The failure modes each one will catch that the others won't
4. **The convergence thesis** — the small overlap where all three say yes

---

# 1. EDWIN "ED" CATMULL — the story judge

### Career

Catmull is not a filmmaker; he is the man who made computer-generated filmmaking physically
possible, and then spent 33 years running the studio that proved it could carry emotion.

- **Technical foundations.** As a graduate student at the University of Utah he invented
  **texture mapping** and the **Z-buffer** — two of the load-bearing algorithms of all 3D
  computer graphics — and produced early computer-animated hand footage that later appeared in
  *Futureworld*. Later work includes subdivision surfaces and the RenderMan lineage.
- **Lucasfilm → Pixar.** He founded and led the Lucasfilm computer graphics division. Steve Jobs
  bought it in 1986 and it became **Pixar**, with Catmull as co-founder.
- **President of Pixar for 33 years**, and concurrently **President of Walt Disney Animation
  Studios for 13 of them** — the only person to have run both. He retired from both roles in
  **2019**.
- **Five Academy Awards** (technical/scientific achievement and the Gordon E. Sawyer Award).
- **2019 ACM A.M. Turing Award** — computing's highest honour — shared with Pat Hanrahan, for
  fundamental contributions to 3D computer graphics and their impact on CGI filmmaking.
- **Author of *Creativity, Inc.***, one of the most-read books on managing creative work.

### What he actually believes (this is the part that matters)

Catmull's public philosophy is unusually consistent and unusually well documented. Four load-bearing ideas:

1. **"From suck to not suck."** His most-quoted framing of the Pixar process: *every* film starts
   bad, and the craft is the honest, iterative work of making it not bad. He does not believe in
   inspiration; he believes in revision. He respects visible evidence of iteration.
2. **The Braintrust and candour.** He built an institution whose entire purpose is people telling
   each other the truth about what isn't working. Candour is, to him, the precondition of quality.
3. **Creativity is problem-solving.** Not magic, not taste — a solvable engineering problem
   approached with humility.
4. **Technology serves story, and the ordering question is genuinely open.** He has spoken
   repeatedly about whether Pixar started with a technology that enabled a story or a story that
   demanded a technology — and treats it as a real question rather than a platitude.

### The last 12 months — and the thing you must understand about his position

- **Joined this jury**, and it has been genuinely controversial. Cartoon Brew and others noted
  he is being promoted as "the man who invented 3D graphics" judging "the first generation of AI
  filmmakers." Creative Bloq covered it as *"reigniting animation's biggest debate."* A large part
  of the animation community regards his participation as lending legitimacy to a technology they
  see as an existential threat to their craft.
- **INMerge Innovation Summit, Baku (Oct 2025)** — spoke on how Pixar "turned the impossible into
  reality" with *Toy Story*.
- **SIGGRAPH Spotlight podcast (Aug 2025)** with Frederic Parke, on pioneering animation and the
  SIGGRAPH legacy.
- Continues to speak on AI in entertainment and the future of storytelling.

**The strategic read — and it is the single most actionable insight in this document:**

> Ed Catmull is currently taking reputational damage for being here. He is 80 years old, has
> nothing left to prove, five Oscars and a Turing Award, and a community of animators publicly
> disappointed in him. **He did not take this seat to crown a good demo reel.** He took it because
> he believes — or wants to believe — that this technology can produce actual cinema.
>
> He needs a winner that vindicates that belief. A film that makes a serious animator say
> *"...okay, that one is real."*
>
> **Give him the film that justifies his presence on the jury and you have his vote.**

That film is not the most technically dazzling one. It is the one with a character whose interior
life is legible, a story that turns, and an ending that means something the beginning didn't.

### What Catmull is watching for

| He rewards | He punishes |
|---|---|
| A protagonist who **wants** something and **changes** | Vibes, mood-reels, "atmosphere" with no spine |
| Setups that pay off | Imagery that means nothing in particular |
| Emotional beats that land as *intended* | Beats that are announced rather than earned |
| Evidence of revision and taste | First-idea execution, however polished |
| Restraint — knowing what to leave out | Everything turned up to 11 |
| A film that would still work as a storyboard | A film that only works because the render is pretty |

# 2. PHEDON PAPAMICHAEL, ASC, GSC — the photography judge

### Career

Two-time Oscar nominee and one of the most respected naturalist cinematographers working.

- **Born in Athens, Greece**; studied filmmaking in Germany. His father, also Phedon Papamichael,
  was a production designer for **John Cassavetes** — *Faces* (1968), *A Woman Under the
  Influence* (1974). He shot his first short film on one of Cassavetes' own cameras. **Naturalism
  is literally inherited.**
- Learned his craft on low-budget independents outside the studio system, in the Roger Corman mould.
- **The James Mangold partnership — seven features:** *Identity*, *Walk the Line*, *3:10 to Yuma*,
  *Knight and Day*, **Ford v Ferrari**, *Le Mans '66*, and **A Complete Unknown** (2024).
- **The Alexander Payne partnership:** *Sideways*, *The Descendants*, and **Nebraska** — the
  black-and-white work that earned him **Academy Award, BAFTA and ASC nominations**.
- **Aaron Sorkin's *The Trial of the Chicago 7*** — the film Higgsfield's own contest page leads
  with — where the challenge was making a courtroom visually alive without visual gimmickry.
- Also: *The Monuments Men*, *The Ides of March*, *Sideways*. He directs as well as shoots.

### His stated philosophy — quote these to yourself while you prompt

> **"I was never a very aggressive 'painter of light.' I always looked first for what the natural
> light was."**

> **"I never want anybody to perceive or feel that I'm lighting something. I never want it to feel
> like there's a craftsman behind it. I always like the photography to just allow us to focus on
> the character, and really not distract from it."**

> **"I always try to maintain a logic to the lighting, with natural inspiration, but heightened."**

He favours **subtle camera moves**, emphasises **composition** over movement, avoids
over-lighting and strong backlight, and deliberately leaves room to discover things on the day. He
and Mangold describe themselves as *"kind of classical Hollywood filmmakers"* who *"love Steven
Spielberg movies."*

### The last 12 months

- **ASC Award nomination for *A Complete Unknown*** (his seventh with Mangold) — celebrated for
  making Timothée Chalamet's Dylan feel photographed rather than staged, using custom lens
  solutions and period-honest light.
- **Cinematography Icon Award 2025**, Evolution Mallorca International Film Festival, presented at
  the opening-night gala, **October 21, 2025**, with an accompanying "Icon Talk."
- ASC Clubhouse Conversation on *A Complete Unknown* with Wally Pfister, ASC.

### The strategic read

**Papamichael is the single most dangerous judge for an AI film, and the most winnable.**

Dangerous because he has spent forty years developing an eye that reads light for *logic*. Every
characteristic AI-video tell is a lighting-logic failure:

- **Two suns.** Shadows falling in contradictory directions in the same frame. He will see this
  in under a second, and it is the most common AI artifact in existence.
- **Sourceless glow.** Light with no visible or implied origin — the "AI sheen." His entire
  professional identity is the opposite of this.
- **Over-lighting.** AI models default to even, flattering, everywhere-light. He explicitly
  avoids it.
- **Plastic skin.** No pores, no asymmetry, no capillary flush.
- **Unmotivated camera movement.** A slow push-in on every shot for no dramatic reason. He looks
  for *subtle* moves that composition justifies.

Winnable because these are all **fixable at the prompt level**, and almost nobody in a field of
15,000 will bother. The technical countermeasures are specified in
[`03-HIGGSFIELD-PLAYBOOK.md`](03-HIGGSFIELD-PLAYBOOK.md) § The Papamichael Filter.

**The single highest-leverage decision you can make for this judge: commit your film to ONE
motivated light source per location, name it in every prompt, and never violate it.** A ghost
light. A helmet lamp. One window. A fire. Firelight and window light are the two most forgiving
sources for AI generation *and* the two most beloved by naturalist DPs. That is a free win.

### What Papamichael is watching for

| He rewards | He punishes |
|---|---|
| One motivated source, consistent shadow direction | Two suns, sourceless glow |
| Skin with pores, asymmetry, real texture | Waxy, retouched, symmetrical faces |
| Camera moves that composition justifies | Push-in on everything; floaty drift |
| Held frames; confidence in a composition | Restless camera hiding weak framing |
| Faces allowed to carry a scene | Cutting away before an actor finishes a thought |
| Colour that reads as a grade, not a filter | Orange-and-teal defaults, LUT-on-everything |

# 3. PAUL W. S. ANDERSON — the spectacle & space judge

### Career

The commercial genre director on the panel, and the one whose taste is most often
underestimated by people who haven't looked closely.

- **Breakthrough:** *Mortal Kombat* (1995) — over $70M, the film that made video-game adaptation
  viable.
- ***Event Horizon*** (1997) — commercially disappointing on release, now a genuine cult classic
  and one of the most respected science-fiction horror films of its decade.
- **The *Resident Evil* franchise** — he created and directed the first six live-action films
  (2002–2016) with **Milla Jovovich**, his longtime collaborator and spouse. Combined with
  *Alien vs Predator* (2004), his films have grossed **over $1 billion worldwide**.
- Also: *Soldier*, *Death Race*, *The Three Musketeers*, *Pompeii*, *Monster Hunter*.
- Longtime producing partner: **Jeremy Bolt**.

### The last 12 months

- ***In the Lost Lands*** — released **March 7, 2025**, adapted from a **George R. R. Martin**
  story, starring **Dave Bautista** and **Milla Jovovich**, budget ~$55M. Notably: a heavily
  stylised, virtual-production-forward film — Anderson is actively working *in* synthetic
  environments right now, not observing them from a distance.
- **Producer on *Protector*** (2025 production, US release March 6, 2026).

### His actual craft — the part that matters enormously here

Anderson is a serious formalist about **3D, space, and depth**, and this is extremely well
documented:

- He was, by critical consensus, **the only major director who used post-*Avatar* 3D
  intelligently** rather than as a post-conversion cash grab — because he shot natively and
  designed for it.
- **He deliberately avoids fast cutting.** His stated 3D approach concentrates on **wide shots,
  visual depth, dollies, rain, and slow motion** — because rapid cutting destroys stereoscopic
  legibility. *He is on record preferring exactly the grammar you want to make.*
- His films are **composed in crisp visual shorthand**: stoic protagonists (usually women)
  meting out violence, figures somersaulting through air, and **tiny figures venturing into
  vast, foreboding spaces**.
- **"Story is often synonymous with setting."** His films have a **locked-room quality** — set
  largely in single, cavernous locations. The Hive. The *Event Horizon*. The arena.

### The strategic read

**Anderson's taste and AI's technical limitations point at the same film.**

He rewards: a single vast coherent location, held wide shots, real depth, deliberate camera
moves, slow motion, atmospherics (rain, dust, smoke, fog), and a small figure dwarfed by space.

AI video is *good at*: a single consistent location, held wide shots with atmospheric motion,
volumetric light through particulate, and slow motion (which also conveniently hides temporal
artifacts).

AI video is *bad at*: complex multi-character choreography, named martial-arts moves, rapid
cutting continuity, and crowds.

**The overlap is nearly perfect.** Building for Anderson is building for the medium's strengths.

One more thing: he is a *practical-effects* director judging a synthetic medium. He will respect
**physical plausibility** — mass, weight, inertia, contact shadows, things that behave like
matter. Floaty AI physics is his equivalent of Papamichael's two suns.

### What Anderson is watching for

| He rewards | He punishes |
|---|---|
| A coherent, legible, awesome **space** | Geography you can't hold in your head |
| Depth staging — foreground / midground / background | Flat, wallpapered backgrounds |
| Scale: small human against vast environment | Everything at medium distance |
| Deliberate dollies, cranes, held wides | Frantic cutting; shaky-cam as substitute for tension |
| Real weight and inertia | Floating props, weightless impacts |
| Atmospherics doing real work | Clean, empty, airless frames |
| Slow motion used as an accent | Slow motion used as a crutch |

# 4. THE CONVERGENCE THESIS

Here is the whole strategy on one page.

|  | Catmull | Papamichael | Anderson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cares about** | Story, character, change | Light logic, restraint, faces | Space, depth, scale, weight |
| **Wants longer holds?** | Yes — performance needs time | Yes — trusts composition | Yes — cutting kills depth |
| **Wants fewer locations?** | Neutral | Yes — one light logic | **Yes — locked-room is his signature** |
| **Wants naturalism?** | Yes, if it serves story | **Emphatically yes** | Yes, on physics |
| **Impressed by tech?** | No | No | Only when invisible |

**All three judges independently want the same film.** They want:

> **One coherent space. One light source. Long held takes. A face allowed to carry a scene.
> Real weight. A story that turns. Cutting used as a weapon, not a default.**

That is not a compromise between three tastes — it is a genuine intersection. And it happens to
be *exactly* the aesthetic you described wanting, and *exactly* what AI video is currently best
at, and — critically — **exactly what a solo creator can actually finish in 24 days**, because
fewer, longer shots means more iterations per shot at a 1.5% acceptance rate.

Four constraints pointing the same direction is not a coincidence you should ignore. It is the plan.

### The corollary — what loses

The modal entry in this contest will be: a 3-minute sci-fi/action montage, 40–60 shots, every one
3–6 seconds, constant camera movement, orange-and-teal grade, a booming trailer score, a portentous
voiceover about humanity and machines, and no character whose name you could recall afterwards.

There will be **thousands** of these. They will all look like each other. Every one of them fails
Catmull (no story), fails Papamichael (no light logic, no faces), and fails Anderson (no coherent
space, cut to pieces).

**Your competitive moat is restraint.** In a field trained to maximise spectacle-per-second,
the film that holds a shot for eighteen seconds and trusts a face will be the only one of its
kind in the room.
