# Reference Films — What to Study and Exactly What to Steal

Curated against our actual problem: **a 3–5 minute, wordless-or-near-wordless,
emotionally decisive short, judged by Edwin Catmull and Phedon Papamichael.**

> **Link note.** Several of these are widely re-uploaded. Where a link is a
> third-party upload rather than the rights-holder's official channel, it is marked.
> Verify before embedding any of these in a public post of ours.

---

## The three to study first

### 1. *The Present* — Jacob Frey (2014) · ~4:19
**▶ https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C_nJJHaNmnY**

A boy is given a three-legged puppy and rejects it — until a reveal reframes
everything. **81 festival awards.**

**Why this one first:** it is the closest existing film to what we need to build. It
executes the exact 3–5 minute structure we are targeting: a small domestic setup, a
character we dislike slightly, a single reveal, and an emotional inversion in the last
20 seconds that makes you re-read the whole film.

**Steal this:**
- **The withheld reveal.** The information that recontextualizes everything is
  physically hidden by framing until the last possible second. Frey shoots *around*
  the fact for four minutes. This is a directing decision, not a writing one — and
  it's directly transferable to *Understudy*.
- **Dislikeable protagonist → earned empathy.** Far more powerful than a sympathetic
  character from frame one, and it buys attention through the middle.
- **Near-zero dialogue.** Carried by behavior and staging.
- **Economy.** No shot exists that isn't setting up the payoff.

### 2. *In a Heartbeat* — Beth David & Esteban Bravo (2017) · ~4:00
**▶ https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uwC5FEhc-20** *(also on CGMeetup:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sAaqLIsOmCE)*

A closeted boy's heart literally leaps out of his chest to chase his crush. A Ringling
student film, Kickstarted for $14,191 — **20 million views in five days, 34M+ total.**

**Why this one:** it is the best available case study in **externalizing an internal
emotion as a physical object.** It is also proof that a 4-minute wordless student film
can out-reach studio output — directly relevant to our 30% engagement problem.

**Steal this:**
- **Make the feeling a character.** The heart *is* the emotion, given a body, so the
  film never has to explain anything. Fire should apply this to whatever our
  through-line is.
- **Zero dialogue, total clarity.** Not one word, not one confusing beat.
- **Physical comedy carrying emotional weight** — buys goodwill before the ache.
- **Virality mechanics.** One-sentence premise, immediately shareable, no context
  required. This is the Wind template.

### 3. *Lights Out* — David F. Sandberg (2013) · ~2:41
**▶ https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UPTCGhOYlJg** *(third-party upload; also
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mVveQvdefVU)*

A woman sees a figure in the hallway when the lights are off. Not when they're on.
Made at home for **$0** by a director and his wife. It became a feature and launched a
Hollywood career.

**Why this one:** it is the purest lesson in **one mechanic, ruthlessly escalated.**
Under three minutes, no exposition, no budget — and it terrified the internet. It is
the antidote to the failure mode this jury explicitly punishes: the flashy reel with
nothing underneath.

**Steal this:**
- **One rule, established in 15 seconds, escalated three times, then broken.** That is
  the entire architecture. It maps perfectly onto *Tidewalker*'s six-minute tide.
- **Constraint as style.** $0 forced a single hallway and a light switch. Our
  constraint is generation consistency — the same logic applies: **lock the location,
  lock the light, escalate the idea.**
- **The hook is instant.** No preamble. Screening survival in its purest form.

## Others worth your time

### *Paperman* — John Kahrs, Disney (2012) · ~6:34
**▶ https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lf1aHC_Ne9o** *(third-party upload; official at
[disneyanimation.com/shorts/paperman](https://disneyanimation.com/shorts/paperman/) and on Disney+)*
Academy Award winner. Wordless, black-and-white, mid-century New York.
**Study for:** monochrome as a discipline — when you remove color you must carry
everything on *light and shape*. Also a hybrid-technique landmark: hand-drawn
expression fused with CG, which is philosophically exactly our problem (human intent
riding on top of a machine pipeline).

### *Piper* — Alan Barillaro, Pixar (2016) · ~6:04
**▶ https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qAIRXIckfMA** *(third-party upload)*
Academy Award winner, Best Animated Short — **the Catmull-era Pixar house style in
concentrate.** A sandpiper chick overcomes fear of water. Not one word.
**Study for:** the emotional arc delivered entirely through *behavior and texture*,
and for wet-sand-and-low-sun photography that is almost a direct reference for
*Tidewalker*. If you want to know what Catmull's taste looks like, it looks like this.

### *Alike* — Daniel Martínez Lara & Rafa Cano Méndez (2016) · ~8:00
**▶ https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kQjtK32mGJQ**
A father and son in a world that drains the color out of people. Goya winner.
**Study for:** **color as the narrative device itself** — saturation literally carries
the plot. A locked, purposeful palette that *shifts at the turn* is one of the most
legible signals of directorial intent available to us, and it reads instantly to a
cinematographer juror.

## The pattern across all of them

Every film on this list does the same five things. Our film must do them too.

1. **One idea.** Not two. A 3–5 minute film that tries to say two things says neither.
2. **The rule is established in the first 15–20 seconds.** No preamble, ever.
3. **Almost no dialogue.** Behavior, staging, and sound carry the meaning.
4. **The turn lands at roughly 60–70%**, leaving room for the consequence to breathe.
5. **The ending recontextualizes the opening.** You want to watch it again immediately.

None of them won on render quality. Every one of them won on a decision.
