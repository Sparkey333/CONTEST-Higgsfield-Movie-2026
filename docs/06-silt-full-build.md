# SILT — Full Snowflake Build (Steps 1–8)

**DarkHearts Ltd.** · Dir. B. L. Barkey · Target runtime **4:22** · 61 shots

---

## Step 1 — Concept Sentence

> **A dam engineer finds the drowned town below still keeping time — and learns the dam has been ringing its bell for sixty-five years.**

*Character:* dam safety engineer. *Engine:* the 18:00 refill order. *Irony:* he maintains the structure that erased his own family, and it turns out the structure has been mourning them louder than he has.

**Controlling idea:** *A man who has spent his life maintaining what destroyed him is freed not by stopping it, but by making it answer him.*

---

## Step 2 — Beat Paragraph

1. Eli Vance, P.E., walks a Colorado reservoir drained to a sixty-five-year low, running a drawdown inspection against an 18:00 refill order.
2. Below the old waterline he finds the street grid of Redstone and, in it, his grandfather's front steps — four concrete treads and a chimney, and nothing else.
3. Sitting there past his own schedule he hears a bell, follows it, and finds that the "hazard marker" he has logged in every report of his career is the top of a forty-foot church tower — and the bell inside it is humming at the exact turbine frequency printed on his own inspection sheet.
4. The radio confirms the gates are opening while he is standing in the tower.
5. At 18:00 he swings the bell himself, one beat ahead of the machine, and then walks out and lets the water come.

## Step 3 — Want / Need / Lie / Ghost

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | Complete the drawdown inspection, sign the report, keep the record clean. |
| **Need** | To stop serving the thing that took from him — and to answer it instead. |
| **Lie** | *"If I maintain it perfectly, it wasn't a crime."* |
| **Ghost** | 1961. His grandfather **Tom Vance** refused the buyout and was carried out of Redstone by sheriff's deputies. He died within the year. Eli grew up to become the engineer who keeps Cabin Creek Dam running. |
| **Arc** | Positive. Penance → address. |
| **Value shift** | **Silence → Sound.** Enters at `−−` (a man who has been quiet about it his whole life). Exits at `++` (he strikes the bell himself). |

### Supporting figures — deliberately thin

- **DISPATCH (V.O. only)** — the radio. Never seen. Represents the institution: reasonable, procedural, and completely deaf. Six lines total.
- **TOM VANCE** — never appears. Present only as four concrete steps, a name on a 1961 plat, and one photograph that we never see the front of.

*In four minutes there is room for exactly one interior. It belongs to Eli.*

## Step 5 — The Turn

**Shot 44. 3:31.**

Eli is standing in the bell chamber. He has the handheld radio and he has the authority — he is the P.E. of record on this structure — to call a drawdown extension and hold the gates for twenty-four hours. Dispatch is waiting for his word. The whole film has been about a man whose entire life is an apology to this valley.

**He sets the radio down on the stone sill and climbs.**

That is the shot. The Lie dies there. He stops trying to *stop* the dam — the penance he has been performing for forty years, one perfect inspection at a time — and starts trying to be **heard over it**. The film is not about saving Redstone. Redstone is gone. It is about a man finally making a sound.

*What the audience sees change:* his hands. For sixty seconds prior they have been full of instruments — clipboard, meter, radio. At Shot 44 they are empty for the first time in the film. Then they are on the rope.

## Step 6 — Look Bible

### The reference
*Nebraska* meets a drawdown inspection report. Available light, working landscape, no beauty that isn't earned by the sun being in that position at that hour.

### Lens language
**Three focal lengths. Never a fourth.**
- **28mm** — the valley, the walk, the silt plain. The man in the landscape.
- **50mm** — Eli working. Hands, instruments, the plat.
- **85mm** — reserved. Four shots in the entire film. His face at 1:52 (the steps), 2:34 (the hum), 3:31 (the turn), 4:06 (the strike). **The 85 is the film's emotional currency and it is spent four times.**

### Light
**Two sources for the whole film. That's it.**
1. **The sun.** Late-afternoon Colorado high-desert, 4:41 PM to 6:00 PM, moving from hard raking side-light to low golden to blue-grey. Every exterior shadow must be consistent with the clock on the radio.
2. **His headlamp.** The only light inside the tower. Single point, hard falloff, everything outside 8 feet is black.

**No third source.** If a shot needs one, the shot is wrong.

### Palette
Desaturated to about 60%. Silt grey-green, drought ochre, wet concrete, oxidized steel. **Hi-vis orange is the only saturated color in the film** and it is always Eli. When the water arrives it brings the second saturated thing: white foam.

### Grain & texture
16mm-weight grain, present in every frame, heavier in the tower interior. Dust in the air on every exterior. **Nothing clean.** The default AI image is glossy; grain and airborne particulate are the cheapest, most effective anti-tells available.

### Camera grammar
Tripod and dolly only. **No handheld, no drift, no floating.** If the camera moves, it moves because a human being pushed it — a slow push, a lateral track, a tilt up. Motion is motivated or it doesn't happen. *(This is also a consistency gift: static and locked-off shots regenerate far more reliably than moving ones.)*

### The three rules you never break
1. **One light source per shot, and you can point at it.**
2. **28 / 50 / 85. Nothing else, ever.**
3. **Orange is only ever Eli.**

## Step 7 — Consistency Sheet

The single hardest problem is character drift. The answer is locked fragments reused **verbatim** plus a shot design that limits exposure.

### Character lock — ELI VANCE
```
LOCKED FRAGMENT — paste verbatim into every shot containing Eli:

A 44-year-old white male civil engineer, 6'0", lean, weathered. Short
grey-brown hair, three days of grey stubble, deep crow's feet, a broken
nose set slightly left. Wearing a scuffed white hard hat with a headlamp
band, a faded hi-vis safety-orange vest over a grey long-sleeve work
shirt with rolled cuffs, dark canvas work pants, and mud-caked boots.
Silt dust on the forearms and the left side of the face. No jewelry;
a pale untanned band on the left ring finger.
```

### Exposure discipline
- **Frontal, clean, well-lit close-ups: 4 shots only** (the 85mm shots). These four get the most generation attempts and the tightest reference.
- **The other 57 shots** hide the face by design — hard hat brim shadow, backlight, three-quarter rear, over-shoulder, silhouette against the silt plain, or headlamp-lit from below with 80% of the face in black.
- This is not a compromise. It is exactly how a DP shoots a lone figure in a working landscape at golden hour, and Papamichael will read it as competence.

### Location locks
```
LOCKED FRAGMENT — RESERVOIR BASIN:
A drained Colorado reservoir basin at record low water. Cracked grey-green
silt in polygonal plates, tire-track-width fissures, scattered bleached
tree stumps cut flat at the 1961 waterline. A pale mineral bathtub ring
40 feet up the canyon walls. Sagebrush and pinyon above the ring, bare
rock below. Hard late-afternoon sun, dust haze, no clouds.

LOCKED FRAGMENT — THE TOWER:
A 40-foot fieldstone church bell tower, mortar leached white, standing
alone on the silt plain. Empty arched belfry openings on four sides.
Interior: a wooden stair spiraling up the inside wall, treads rotted
and black, iron ladder rungs above. A single bronze bell, green with
patina, silt-caked, hanging from an oak headstock.
```

### The plat
The 1961 survey plat Eli carries is a **recurring insert** — it appears in 6 shots. Generate it once, lock it, and reuse the same asset. It is the film's only exposition device and it does the work of two pages of dialogue.

## Production Notes

**The four expensive shots.** 22 (the steps reveal), 40 (the bell), 56 (the strike), 60 (the refill wide). These get 3× the generation budget of anything else. Shot 60 is the hook clip.

**The four 85mm shots** (25, 35, 50, 58) are the film. If those four faces don't hold, nothing else matters. Lock the character reference on these first, before any other production.

**Shot 3 → Shot 4** is a hard cut from black-and-underwater-silence to blinding sun and cicadas. It's the loudest edit in the film and it happens at 22 seconds. That is deliberate: it buys you the juror's attention on submission #300.

**What is deliberately absent.** No flashback to 1961. No photograph of Tom Vance. No dialogue from Eli at all — he never speaks. The only voice in the film is a radio that isn't listening. *If you are tempted to add a flashback, don't. The four concrete steps are the flashback.*
