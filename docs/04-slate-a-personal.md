# Slate A — Personal Fusion

Five concepts built from the seams in `02-creative-dna.md`. Each is taken to **Snowflake Step 4**.

These are chosen for *authorial truth* first — they are the films only you can make. Contest optimization is Slate B's job. Several of these graduate.

---

## A1 · TAILWATER

**Step 1 — Concept Sentence**
> A dam engineer at record-low water walks down into the drowned town his family was moved out of.

**Step 2 — Beat Paragraph**
1. Eli Vance, a dam safety engineer, is sent to inspect a Colorado reservoir drained to record low by drought, with a refill scheduled at 18:00.
2. Walking the exposed lakebed with a 1961 survey plat, he finds the street grid of Redstone — the town condemned to build the dam his grandfather refused to leave.
3. He finds the house: a chimney, four front steps, and nothing else — and sits down on his grandfather's steps in the mud, on the clock, doing nothing.
4. The refill call comes over the radio while he is still sitting there.
5. He walks out ahead of the water and signs the report, and the last shot is the steps going under.

**Step 3 — Want / Need / Lie / Ghost**
- **Want:** complete the inspection, sign the report, keep the job clean.
- **Need:** to stop atoning to a structure that cannot absolve him.
- **Lie:** *"If I maintain it perfectly, it wasn't a crime."*
- **Ghost:** his grandfather was carried out of Redstone by sheriff's deputies in 1961 and died within the year; Eli grew up to become the engineer who keeps the dam.

**Step 4 — Runtime Map (4:15)**

| Beat | Time | Shots | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:50 | 9 | Drought light, truck, boat ramp ending in dirt, radio establishes the 18:00 clock |
| 2 | 0:50–1:55 | 14 | The walk down. Silt plain. Foundations, a mailbox, the grid resolving into a town |
| 3 | 1:55–2:50 | 11 | The house. The steps. He sits. Held. |
| 4 | 2:50–3:30 | 9 | Radio. He does not move. Then he does. |
| 5 | 3:30–4:15 | 10 | The walk out, the water arriving, the steps going under |

**Verdict.** The most emotionally honest thing on either slate, and the closest to your actual life. Papamichael would rate it very highly and Catmull would rate it highly. **Anderson would be bored** — there is no engine after the reveal and no set piece. *It is 80% of a winner and it is missing exactly one idea.* Slate B supplies it (see **B1 · SILT**).

## A2 · MEMORY OF WATER

**Step 1 — Concept Sentence**
> A hydrologist proves a river remembers the day a boy drowned in it, and must decide whether to publish.

**Step 2 — Beat Paragraph**
1. Dr. Ana Reyes runs acoustic surveys on a Colorado river bend, chasing an anomaly in the return signal that shouldn't be there.
2. Played back at the right frequency, the bend reproduces a fixed, repeating twenty-second event: shouting, splashing, and then nothing.
3. She matches it against a 2003 coroner's record and realizes she is listening to a nine-year-old drown, on loop, forever, and that the water will keep doing it whether or not she is there.
4. She takes the recording to the boy's mother, who is now seventy and has stopped asking.
5. She erases the file, and the last shot is the river doing it again with nobody listening.

**Step 3 — Want / Need / Lie / Ghost**
- **Want:** publish the anomaly; the discovery of her career.
- **Need:** to accept that some knowledge is a wound you inflict on other people.
- **Lie:** *"Truth is always worth what it costs someone else."*
- **Ghost:** her own paper eight years ago named a dam operator, correctly, and he took his life.

**Step 4 — Runtime Map (4:30)**

| Beat | Time | Shots | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:55 | 10 | Field survey, hydrophones, spectrogram, the anomaly |
| 2 | 0:55–1:50 | 12 | The playback. The twenty seconds. Her face. |
| 3 | 1:50–2:45 | 10 | Archive, the record, the realization |
| 4 | 2:45–3:45 | 11 | The mother's kitchen. The choice. |
| 5 | 3:45–4:30 | 8 | Erase. The river. Nobody there. |

**Verdict.** The most *beautiful* concept here and a natural fit for a composer — the sound design **is** the plot. Strong on Catmull, very strong on Storytelling & Creativity. But the climax is a woman deciding not to do something, which is hard to photograph and gives Anderson nothing. Also: the drowning-child audio is a real risk of reading as exploitative if handled a millimeter wrong. **Hold as a future DarkHearts film, not a contest entry.**

## A3 · CEPHAS — FIRST DREAM

**Step 1 — Concept Sentence**
> A boy who dreams the Titans' war must decide whether waking up is a betrayal.

**Step 2 — Beat Paragraph**
1. On the tropical island of Cephas, Ammon trains to become a Guardian and hides the dreams that have started arriving nightly.
2. In the dream he is *inside* the Titans' war over the Creation Stones, and he is not a witness — he is being *addressed*.
3. He realizes the dream is not showing him the past; the last hidden Stone is showing him where it is, and it is under the island he has lived on his whole life.
4. He tells no one and goes to the water alone.
5. He surfaces holding something warm, and the island's birds all go silent at once.

**Step 3 — Want / Need / Lie / Ghost**
- **Want:** to be chosen as a Guardian, properly, by the normal path.
- **Need:** to accept a calling that will cost him the normal path forever.
- **Lie:** *"If I earn it the ordinary way, I get to stay who I am."*
- **Ghost:** the dreams began the night his father's ship didn't come back.

**Step 4 — Runtime Map (4:40)**

| Beat | Time | Shots | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:55 | 11 | Cephas by day. Training. The hiding. |
| 2 | 0:55–2:00 | 15 | **The dream.** Titans, Stones, scale, address. The set piece. |
| 3 | 2:00–2:50 | 10 | Waking. The recognition. The map in his own memory. |
| 4 | 2:50–3:40 | 9 | The walk to the water. Alone. |
| 5 | 3:40–4:40 | 10 | The dive. The warm thing. The silence. |

**Verdict.** The **strategic** play, not the artistic one — you are a published novelist filming your own book, and that is a press release that writes itself (15% Social Engagement). Anderson gets his set piece in Beat 2. The risk is real though: adaptations of chapter one tend to play as **trailers for something else**, which is fatal with Catmull. It survives only if Beat 5 lands as a genuine ending rather than a hook. Kept in Slate B as **B4**, downgraded to third priority on producibility grounds — Titans, a tropical island, water, *and* a crowd is the most expensive shot list on either slate.

## A4 · ROLL

**Step 1 — Concept Sentence**
> Every time he taps, the roll restarts — and his opponent has been his own shoulder all along.

**Step 2 — Beat Paragraph**
1. A no-gi grappler slaps hands with a training partner in an empty gym and gets caught in ninety seconds.
2. He taps — and is instantly back at the handshake, same light, same mat, same everything.
3. Six loops in, sweating, furious, he notices his partner is defending the same shoulder every single time, and that it's *his* bad shoulder, the one he never rehabbed.
4. On the last loop he stops attacking, gives up position, and lets himself be taken — and the shoulder does not go.
5. The gym lights are off and he is alone on the mat, breathing, and it is a different day.

**Step 3 — Want / Need / Lie / Ghost**
- **Want:** to win the roll.
- **Need:** to stop defending an injury he has organized his whole game around.
- **Lie:** *"If I never expose it, it never happened."*
- **Ghost:** the shoulder went two years ago and he trained through it instead of fixing it.

**Step 4 — Runtime Map (4:20)**

| Beat | Time | Shots | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:45 | 10 | Empty gym, one light, the slap, the roll, the tap |
| 2 | 0:45–1:30 | 12 | Reset. Reset. Reset. Escalating cuts, shortening loops |
| 3 | 1:30–2:30 | 13 | The noticing. The shoulder. His own face understanding. |
| 4 | 2:30–3:35 | 12 | The final roll. He gives it up. Slow, quiet, total. |
| 5 | 3:35–4:20 | 7 | Dark gym. Alone. Breathing. Different day. |

**Verdict.** Enormously smart on production grounds and I want to flag why: **the loop turns AI's worst weakness into the film's style.** Repeated near-identical shots are exactly what these models produce anyway; here the repetition is *the point*, and small drift between loops reads as intentional unease rather than as a defect. One location, one light, two bodies, no dialogue. Anderson gets a fight. Catmull gets a real arc with a genuine turn. Papamichael gets a single hard practical over a mat.

**This is the best-engineered idea on Slate A.** Promoted to Slate B as **B2**.

## A5 · THE DOG AT THE DOOR

**Step 1 — Concept Sentence**
> A shiba waits at the door of a house being taken apart around him.

**Step 2 — Beat Paragraph**
1. A shiba inu lies in a hallway facing a front door, in a house full of boxes.
2. Over days — light moving, boxes leaving, furniture going, then the furniture's shadows on the wallpaper — the house empties around the dog, who does not move from the spot.
3. Movers carry out a recliner, and the dog finally stands, walks to where the chair was, and lies down in the empty shape of it instead.
4. A woman crouches in the doorway with a leash and waits, not calling, just waiting, until the dog comes.
5. The last shot is the empty house, and the shape in the carpet where the chair stood.

**Step 3 — Want / Need / Lie / Ghost**
- **Want:** for him to come home.
- **Need:** nothing. *(This is deliberate — the dog does not arc. The **audience** arcs. That is the whole design.)*
- **Lie:** *"He is late."*
- **Ghost:** he is not late.

**Step 4 — Runtime Map (4:00)**

| Beat | Time | Shots | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:40 | 8 | The hallway. The door. The dog. Boxes. No explanation. |
| 2 | 0:40–2:10 | 16 | Time passing in one geometry. Light arcs. The house empties. |
| 3 | 2:10–2:55 | 8 | The recliner leaves. The dog moves — for the first time. |
| 4 | 2:55–3:35 | 7 | The woman. The doorway. The waiting. |
| 5 | 3:35–4:00 | 4 | Empty house. The shape in the carpet. |

**Verdict.** **This is the Audience Choice film.** No dialogue, no language barrier, universally legible, devastating in four minutes, and by a wide margin the cheapest thing on either slate to produce — one hallway, one dog, one lighting geometry, changing only in light and set dressing. That constraint is *also* an AI-consistency dream: locking a single camera position across sixteen shots is trivially achievable, and the film's whole aesthetic is "the camera never moves."

Audience Choice pays **$100,000** — the same as third place — and is a completely different competition from the jury prize. Promoted to Slate B as **B3**, and built out in full in `docs/12-dog-full-build.md` (runtime revised 4:00 → **4:12** to sit inside the brief's target band; shot count unchanged at 43).
