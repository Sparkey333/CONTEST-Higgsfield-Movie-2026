## CAN WE SUBMIT MORE THAN ONE FILM?

**Yes. Entries are unlimited.** Two conditions:

1. **Each entry must be a standalone film.** You cannot cut one story into five chapters and submit five entries.
2. **One prize per participant.** If two of your films land in the top placements, you take the higher one and the other slot cascades to someone else.

> **What that changes:** a slate is still worth running — two films means two chances to win *something*, and the odds of at least one placing are higher than either alone. But the prizes do **not** stack, so the second film has to genuinely cost you very little. It cannot come out of the flagship's quality.
>
> **Unresolved:** whether **Audience Choice** counts as a "placement" under the one-prize cap or sits outside it. If it's outside, the SILT + DOG slate is excellent. If it's inside, the second film is insurance, not upside. **Verify this — it is question #6 in the brief.**

# AVOID

## Hard disqualifiers — these end your entry

- **Copyrighted IP.** No movie or game characters, no brand logos, no recognizable trademarks, no visible product design.
- **Licensed music.** Original or royalty-free only. Every audio source documented — your project files go public and become inspectable.
- **NSFW / pornographic content.**
- **Political statements.**
- **Religious statements.** ⚠️ *Read this one carefully given the DarkHearts material — a Goetia, Enoch, or Hekhalot film is squarely in the blast radius. A church bell tower as a location is set dressing; a film that argues a theological position is not. Know which one you are making.*
- **Under 3:00 runtime.**
- **Missing or altered watermark.**
- **Non-standalone entries.**
- Serious violations can mean a **permanent ban from future contests.** This is not worth testing.

## Craft killers — these lose the jury

**The single most common submission in this contest will be a fake trailer for an imaginary blockbuster. It dies with all three jurors. Do not make it.**

- **Wall-to-wall score from frame one.** Silence is the most underused tool in the format.
- **Voiceover explaining the theme.** If the film needs narration to mean something, it doesn't mean it.
- **Text cards doing the story's work.** One end card is a signature. Four is a screenplay you failed to shoot.
- **A montage instead of a scene.** Montage is a summary. Juries score scenes.
- **Light from nowhere.** Ambient fill with no source. *(Papamichael's whole career is the opposite of this.)*
- **Teal-and-orange grade.** The default AI palette. Pick a real one.
- **A different focal length every shot.** Three lenses, assigned jobs, no exceptions.
- **Floating, drifting camera.** If it moves, a human pushed it.
- **Plastic skin, glossy surfaces, no grain.** Grain and airborne particulate are the cheapest anti-tells available.
- **Character face drift.** Lock the fragments; hide the face by design in most shots.
- **Six characters in four minutes.** There is room for exactly one interior.
- **Dialogue that states the subtext.**
- **Slow motion as a substitute for meaning.**
- **Ending on a hook for a feature that doesn't exist.** Catmull's test is whether *this* film ended.
- **No nameable turn.** If you can't point at the shot where the character changed, you have a mood.

## Production traps

- **Generating before look dev.** Six test shots first, always.
- **Working in shot order instead of beat order.** A slip should cost you a beat, not the film.
- **Leaving the hard shots for last.** If the set piece fails on Aug 25 you have no film. Test it Aug 12.
- **Not locking prompt fragments.** Paste them verbatim. Do not "improve" them between shots.
- **Not budgeting keep rate.** *Hell Grind* kept 253 of 16,181. Plan generations, not shots.
- **Submitting on Aug 31.** No campaign runway, and every contest server falls over in the last six hours.

## Campaign misses — this is 30% of the score

- **Uploading and going silent.**
- **Dumping project files instead of curating them.** Title it as a tutorial, not a folder.
- **Harvesting a hook clip instead of designing one.** Build the 12-second shot on purpose.
- **No hook inside the first 25 seconds.** A juror is on submission #300.

# BEATS TO HIT
## The Hero's Journey in 5 Minutes — nine beats

*Percentages scale to any runtime. Columns show 4:00, 4:30, and 5:00 cuts.*

| # | Beat | % | 4:00 | 4:30 | 5:00 | The rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ordinary World** | 7 | 0:17 | 0:19 | 0:21 | **One image, not a sequence.** One shot that says who they are and what's normal. |
| 2 | **Call to Adventure** | 9 | 0:22 | 0:24 | 0:27 | **An event, not information.** Something *happens*. Nobody explains anything. |
| 3 | **Refusal** | 5 | 0:12 | 0:14 | 0:15 | **A held look, not a scene.** Fifteen seconds maximum. Hesitation, then motion. |
| 4 | **The Mentor** | 7 | 0:17 | 0:19 | 0:21 | **An object, not a person.** A short has no room for a mentor character. Give them a thing that knows something. |
| 5 | **Crossing the Threshold** | 8 | 0:19 | 0:22 | 0:24 | **Irreversible.** After this shot they cannot go back to beat 1. Make the audience feel the door shut. |
| 6 | **Approach / The One Test** | 19 | 0:46 | 0:51 | 0:57 | **One obstacle, not three.** "Tests, allies and enemies" collapses to a single physical approach. |
| 7 | **THE ORDEAL** | 22 | 0:53 | 0:59 | 1:06 | **The set piece. The biggest block in the film.** This is what Anderson screenshots. |
| 8 | **Reward + Road Back** | 12 | 0:29 | 0:32 | 0:36 | **Merge them.** The reward is knowledge, not an object. The road back is short. |
| 9 | **Return with the Elixir** | 11 | 0:26 | 0:30 | 0:33 | **The change must be visible.** Not stated, not narrated — *seen*. |

### The seven rules that make it work

1. **Cut the sequence, keep the image.** Every beat that is a *phase* in a novel is a *shot* in a short.
2. **The mentor is an object.** A letter, a map, a survey plat, a leash, a photograph. It carries the exposition a character would have spoken.
3. **One test, not three.** Tests/Allies/Enemies is a whole act in a feature. Here it is one obstacle and the audience is the ally.
4. **Ordeal and Resurrection merge.** In a short, surviving the ordeal *is* the resurrection. Two beats, one sequence.
5. **The Ordeal owns a fifth of the runtime.** If your set piece is shorter than beat 6, you built the wrong film.
6. **The Return is not a wrap-up — it's the proof.** Show one thing they now do differently. That single shot is the whole arc's receipt.
7. **Beat 1 and beat 9 should rhyme.** Same place, same framing, same object — one thing changed. It is the cheapest and most reliable ending in the format.

### Where it's allowed to break

- **In medias res.** Open on a fragment of beat 7, then hard-cut to beat 1. Buys attention in the first 20 seconds. *(SILT does this.)*
- **A moral ordeal.** Beat 7 can be a decision rather than a fight — a character declining to use power they hold. Harder to photograph, higher ceiling.
- **A flat protagonist.** Some shorts arc the *audience* instead of the character. Legitimate and powerful — but then stop calling it a Hero's Journey and stop trying to force beat 9. *(THE DOG AT THE DOOR is this. Do not map it to this table.)*

## THE DAILY CHECK

Ask these three every day of production. If any answer is no, stop generating and fix it.

> **1.** Can I say in one sentence what the character wants? *(Catmull)*
> **2.** Can I point at where the light in this shot is coming from? *(Papamichael)*
> **3.** What is the one shot people will screenshot? *(Anderson)*
