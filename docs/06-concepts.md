# The Three Concepts

Three candidate films, each engineered against the rubric, the jury, and the
compliance gate. All three are **wordless or near-wordless** — a deliberate choice.
Dialogue is where AI film visibly breaks (lip-sync, vocal performance, the uncanny
beat before a line), and wordless emotional storytelling is precisely the register
Edwin Catmull spent forty years building at Pixar.

All three clear the hard "no" list: no IP, no logos, no licensed music, no NSFW, **no
political content, no religious content.** All three are invented worlds with
universal human stakes — the safe harbor.

---

## CONCEPT A — *Understudy*

> **Logline:** An aging puppeteer's marionette begins, quietly, to move on its own —
> steadying the old man's shaking hands so the show can go on.

**Runtime target:** 4:00 · **Register:** intimate, warm, devastating

### The film
A tiny theater, one row of empty seats. An old puppeteer performs a nightly show to
nobody. His hands shake. The marionette stumbles. Over three performances, the puppet
begins compensating — catching its own fall, hitting its mark, gently pulling a string
back into place. The old man doesn't notice. Or pretends not to. On the final night he
doesn't arrive. The marionette performs the whole show alone, takes its bow to the
empty room, walks upstage, and lies down carefully beside the coat he left on the chair.

### Why it wins

| Criterion | The case |
|---|---|
| **Storytelling (25%)** | This is a Catmull film in its bones — an object with a soul, inverted. *Toy Story* asked whether toys love us; this asks whether they'd carry us. One idea, one turn, earned ending. |
| **Cinematic (25%)** | Theater lighting is *motivated by definition*: one practical spotlight, warm key, deep falloff into black. Papamichael can read the light source in every frame. Enormously controllable. |
| **Technical (20%)** | **Single location** — the strongest continuity advantage available. Two characters. Locked palette. |
| **Engagement (30%)** | The premise is a one-sentence hook that travels. "The puppet starts holding *him* up." |

### Production reality
- ✅ One set, one light, two characters → continuity is *tractable*
- ✅ Miniature scale suits AI generation
- 🔴 **Hands.** Puppeteering is entirely hands, and hands remain AI's worst subject.
  **Mitigation:** silhouette, shallow focus, shoot past the hands to the puppet, cut
  on the shake rather than holding on fingers. Phedon owns this from day one.
- 🟡 Old man's face consistency across ~25 shots → reference-locked identity required

**Risk rating: MEDIUM** (hands) · **Ceiling: HIGHEST**

## CONCEPT B — *The Keeper*

> **Logline:** A man tends a vast, ancient machine alone — and we discover its only
> purpose is to produce a single flower, once a year, for one grave.

**Runtime target:** 4:30 · **Register:** awe → intimacy

### The film
Open on scale: brass, steam, cathedral-sized gearworks, shafts of dust-lit light. One
small figure servicing it with total devotion. Escalating scale — bigger halls, bigger
machines, the sense of something civilizational. Then the machine completes its cycle,
and from a tiny aperture at the end of all that apparatus: **one flower.** He carries
it up and out into daylight, walks a short distance, and lays it on a grave. Final
shot: he turns and goes back down. He will do this again next year.

### Why it wins

| Criterion | The case |
|---|---|
| **Storytelling (25%)** | The whole film is one reveal, and the reveal recontextualizes everything. Scale used as misdirection for intimacy. |
| **Cinematic (25%)** | God-rays through dust, brass and shadow, volumetric depth. **This is the most photographable concept of the three** — Papamichael's register exactly. |
| **Technical (20%)** | Machinery is what AI generation does *best* — hard surfaces, no anatomy, no lip-sync. Lowest technical-failure risk of the three. |
| **Engagement (30%)** | Visually spectacular stills for social. Every frame is a poster. |

### Production reality
- ✅ **Lowest AI-failure risk** — machines, not faces or hands
- ✅ Spectacle sells the first 15 seconds → strong Screening survival
- 🟡 Single human character; needs identity lock but has limited close-ups
- 🟡 **Braintrust's warning:** "lone figure tending vast machinery" flirts with the
  crowded dieselpunk/solarpunk lane. Differentiation must come from the *ending*,
  not the production design.

**Risk rating: LOW** · **Ceiling: HIGH**

## CONCEPT C — *Tidewalker*

> **Logline:** In a town where the sea withdraws for exactly six minutes each day, a
> boy sprints across the exposed seabed to retrieve something before the water returns.

**Runtime target:** 3:45 · **Register:** kinetic, ticking clock, breathless

### The film
A coastal town. A bell rings. The entire sea *pulls back*, exposing miles of glistening
seabed — wrecks, stranded things, a whole hidden landscape. Everyone stays behind the
line. A boy doesn't. He runs. Cross-cut with the bell, the tide line, the townspeople.
He reaches what he came for, and turns — the water is already coming. The run back is
the action sequence. He makes it. And what he was carrying is revealed: something
small, and worth it.

### Why it wins

| Criterion | The case |
|---|---|
| **Storytelling (25%)** | A ticking clock *is* structure. The premise is instantly legible with zero exposition. |
| **Cinematic (25%)** | Low sun on wet sand, mirror reflections, vast negative space, a wall of returning water. Wide-landscape lyricism — Papamichael shot *Nebraska*; he reads this language natively. |
| **Technical (20%)** | Water simulation is demanding, but a **single running child** is far easier to hold consistent than a face in dialogue. |
| **Engagement (30%)** | 🏆 **Strongest hook of the three.** "The sea pulls back for six minutes a day" stops a scroll instantly. Best Audience Choice candidate. |

### Production reality
- ✅ **Doubles as an entry to "Make Your Action Scene"** ($500K, same Aug 31 deadline)
  — the run-back sequence is a standalone action scene. `[VERIFY]` cross-entry rules.
- ✅ Most viral premise → best Audience Choice ($100K) positioning
- 🔴 Returning-tide shots are the hardest generation in any of the three concepts
- 🟡 Exteriors mean weather/light continuity discipline across many shots

**Risk rating: MEDIUM-HIGH** · **Ceiling: HIGH** · **Engagement ceiling: HIGHEST**

## Council recommendation — REVISED after the third juror

> **2026-08-08 — strategy change.** The official page confirms a **third judge: Paul
> W. S. Anderson** (*Mortal Kombat*, *Resident Evil*, *Alien vs. Predator*, *Event
> Horizon*). Our original ranking assumed a two-person jury of Catmull (story) and
> Papamichael (light) and recommended the quietest, most intimate concept. **That was
> a one-vote-of-three strategy.** Anderson has spent thirty years holding audience
> attention against its will. A beautiful static film loses his vote outright.

### Scoring against all three jurors

| | **Catmull** *(heart)* | **Papamichael** *(craft)* | **Anderson** *(propulsion)* | Engagement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Understudy** | ★★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★ | ★★★★ |
| **B · The Keeper** | ★★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★★★ |
| **C · Tidewalker** | ★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★★★★★ |

*Understudy* is still the best pure-Catmull film we have — and it is the only one that
scores **two stars** with a juror. A marionette film with no propulsion is exactly the
festival short Anderson has professionally spent his career being the opposite of.

### Revised play

- **🥇 PRIMARY: C · *Tidewalker*.** The only concept that scores ★★★★ or better with
  **all three** jurors. It has a real emotional core (what he's retrieving), genuine
  photographic beauty (low sun, wet sand, mirror reflections), and a **built-in
  ticking clock with an action set-piece** — Anderson's exact register. It also
  carries the strongest hook for the $100K Audience Choice and **double-dips into
  *Make Your Action Scene*** ($500K, same deadline).
  **Three independent reasons now point at the same film.**
- **🥈 SECONDARY: A · *Understudy*.** Entries are unlimited. This stays in the
  portfolio as the pure-heart play — if the shortlist skews toward Catmull's taste, it
  is our best entry. Build it second, from surplus craft.
- **🛟 RESERVE: B · *The Keeper*.** Lowest technical risk and the best balance across
  all three jurors after C. Pivot here if *Tidewalker*'s water simulation proves
  unrecoverable in early tests.

### What this changes in production

*Tidewalker*'s risk profile is water and exteriors, not hands. That moves the hardest
generation problem from **anatomy** (unsolvable) to **simulation and continuity**
(solvable with reference chaining and shot design). Phedon should test a returning-tide
shot **before Gate 1** — if it can't be made to work, we pivot to B immediately rather
than discovering it at day 15.
