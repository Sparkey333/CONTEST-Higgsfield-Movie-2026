# 06 — The Wargame: Scoring Harness

A repeatable rubric for grading any concept twice: **as a short film**, and **as a thing these
three specific people will watch on a specific afternoon in late September.**

Those are different questions and a concept can pass one and fail the other.

---

# 1. THE RUBRIC

## Part 1 — Film Grade (0–50)

Judge-blind. Would this be good if it were made in live action by competent people?

| # | Criterion | Pts | The question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Want & Turn** | 10 | Is there a clear want, and a real turn where the film becomes about something else? |
| 2 | **Emotional payload** | 10 | Does the ending cost the protagonist something the audience feels? |
| 3 | **Originality** | 10 | Against 15,000 entries — has the jury seen this shape before *today*? |
| 4 | **Layered truth** | 10 | Does it say ≥2 things at once, without stating either? |
| 5 | **The last image** | 10 | Does the final shot recontextualise, land, and stay? |

## Part 2 — Judge Fit (0–30)

| Judge | Pts | What is actually being scored |
|---|---|---|
| **Catmull** | 12 | Story spine, character interiority, restraint, evidence of revision, "is it real cinema" |
| **Papamichael** | 10 | Single motivated light source, faces, held frames, naturalism, no AI tells |
| **Anderson** | 8 | Coherent awesome space, depth, scale, weight, atmospherics, held wides |

*Weighting rationale:* Catmull carries the most weight because he is the story authority, the most
senior figure on the panel, and the one with the strongest personal stake in the outcome (see
[`01-JUDGES-DOSSIER.md`](01-JUDGES-DOSSIER.md) § 1). Anderson carries the least not because he
matters less, but because his criteria are the easiest to satisfy incidentally — a good space and
a held wide come free with the house style.

## Part 3 — Make-ability (0–20)

**24 days, ~1.5% acceptance rate, one person.** This is not a tiebreaker; it is a gate.

| # | Criterion | Pts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Location count** (1 loc = 5, 2 = 3, 3+ = 1) | 5 |
| 2 | **Character sheet burden** (1 = 5, 2 = 3, 3+ = 1) | 5 |
| 3 | **Light simplicity** (one source, high contrast, lots of black = 5) | 5 |
| 4 | **Low-risk shot profile** (no crowds/fights/reflections/doubles) | 5 |

## Part 4 — Multipliers

Applied to the total:

| Modifier | × | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Audience Choice potential** | ×1.10 | Has a hook that survives 15 seconds on a feed |
| **Meta-risk penalty** | ×0.85 | The film comments on AI/replacement — jury may bounce |
| **Dialogue-heavy penalty** | ×0.90 | >8 spoken lines — AI's weakest capability |
| **Crowd penalty** | ×0.85 | Any shot requiring >5 people |

**Total: 100 points before multipliers.**

# 2. SCORES — the six concepts

| | Film /50 | Catmull /12 | Papa /10 | Anderson /8 | Make /20 | Raw | Mult | **Final** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Vantage** | 41 | 10 | 9 | 8 | 19 | 87 | ×1.10 | **95.7** |
| **C. Salvage** | 43 | 11 | 9 | 8 | 17 | 88 | ×1.00 | **88.0** |
| **B. Tenant** | 44 | 11 | 10 | 6 | 19 | 90 | ×0.97* | **87.3** |
| **F. Ghost Light** | 42 | 11 | 10 | 5 | 20 | 88 | ×0.85 | **74.8** |
| **E. The Listening** | 45 | 11 | 9 | 7 | 11 | 83 | ×0.85 | **70.6** |
| **D. Second Language** | 40 | 11 | 10 | 2 | 18 | 81 | ×0.90 | **72.9** |

\* Tenant: no clean hook for Audience Choice (×1.00), slight dialogue credit for being nearly silent.

### Reading the table

**Vantage wins on balance, not on peaks.** Tenant and The Listening score higher as *films*, but
Vantage is the only concept that scores ≥8 with all three judges while remaining maximally
make-able. In a three-person jury, the film with no weak axis beats the film with one great axis
and one bad one — juries converge on the film nobody objects to.

**Salvage is the closest challenger** and arguably the better film. Its 17/20 make-ability
reflects the visor problem (see [`05-STORY-CONCEPTS.md`](05-STORY-CONCEPTS.md) § C). Solve the
face-visibility issue and it goes to 19 and takes the lead.

**The Listening has the highest film grade of all six (45/50) and the lowest make-ability
(11/20).** This is the classic trap: the best idea you cannot execute at quality in the time
available. Reducing the rescue team from twenty to three lifts make-ability to ~17 and the crowd
penalty disappears — **that single change moves it to ~85 and makes it a genuine contender.**
Worth doing.

**Ghost Light is the highest-variance play in the set.** The ×0.85 meta-risk multiplier is an
average across outcomes, not a prediction. It genuinely could take first place with Catmull, and
it genuinely could read as manipulative to a jury defending its own legitimacy. If your instinct
says the meta-layer is the point, override the multiplier — but do it knowingly.

**Second Language is dragged down almost entirely by one judge.** 40/50 as a film, 23/30 on the
two judges it fits, and 2/8 on Anderson. It is the clearest illustration of why the harness scores
judges separately: a concept can be excellent and still be structurally wrong for the room.

# 3. PREDICTED JUDGE COMMENTARY

What each judge would plausibly *say* about the front-runner, written as the note they'd leave.

### On **Vantage**

> **Catmull:** *"There's a real decision in it, and the decision costs her. The last image sends
> you back to the first one. I believe the person, which is the only thing I ever care about.
> I'd have wanted one more moment of her before the fire turns — we need to like her more before
> we're asked to lose her."*

> **Papamichael:** *"The lighting change is the story. That's a real cinematographic idea, not a
> filter. The tower interior holds up because there's one source and it behaves. Watch the glass —
> anything shot toward the windows at an angle is where it'll fall apart."*

> **Anderson:** *"Great box. She's small and the thing outside is enormous and you never lose the
> geography. The ember work is beautiful. Give me one more genuinely wide shot from outside the
> tower — I want to see how alone that building is."*

### On the modal competitor entry (the film you're beating)

> **Catmull:** *"Extremely accomplished images. I could not tell you what happened or to whom."*

> **Papamichael:** *"Every shot is lit from everywhere. There's no time of day. Nobody made a
> choice."*

> **Anderson:** *"It's cut so fast I never worked out where anyone was standing."*

**Those three sentences are the target you are avoiding.** Print them.

# 4. THE TWO-GAMES PROBLEM

There are two separate prizes with different judges:

| | Jury prizes ($800k) | Audience Choice ($100k) |
|---|---|---|
| Decided by | Catmull, Papamichael, Anderson | A crowd |
| Rewards | Restraint, story, light logic, held frames | Hook, spectacle-per-second, shareability |
| Fails on | Empty spectacle | Slow openings, ambiguity, quiet endings |
| Watch conditions | Attentive, full runtime, large screen | Distracted, first 15s, small screen |

**Do not try to win both with the same cut.** Resolve it this way:

1. **The film is built for the jury. Non-negotiable.** 80% of the money is there, and a film built
   for a crowd loses the jury far more decisively than the reverse.
2. **The Audience Choice campaign is a separate deliverable:** a 15–30 second vertical cut, built
   from your strongest single image and your one action burst, posted for reach. It advertises the
   film; it is not the film.
3. **The only concession the film itself makes to the crowd is the cold open** — the "loaded
   stillness" solution in [`02-DIRECTING-DOCTRINE.md`](02-DIRECTING-DOCTRINE.md) § 6, which
   satisfies both audiences without compromising either.

# 6. THE KILL CRITERIA

Be honest early. A concept should be **killed or restructured** if any of these are true — and it
is far cheaper to kill it here than on day 12.

- ❌ You cannot state the turn in one sentence
- ❌ It needs more than 3 locations
- ❌ It needs more than 2 locked character sheets
- ❌ The light source changes without a story reason
- ❌ It requires a fight, a crowd, or a vehicle chase to work
- ❌ The ending explains itself in dialogue
- ❌ You cannot name the last image
- ❌ Make-ability scores below 12/20 and you are working alone

### The single most important question

> **If you removed every generated image and told this story out loud in ninety seconds, would
> anyone care?**

If yes, the technology will make it better. If no, the technology will make it *longer*, and Ed
Catmull will know inside thirty seconds.
