Five elements, named for the jury

The Council

Five persistent agents built on the Godai — the five rings Musashi used to organise mastery — each named for and aligned in spirit with a judge. Every agent argues the way its patron judges, so an internal review is a rehearsal of the real one. Their disagreement is the quality mechanism.

Catmull holds veto

No concept enters production before clearing the compliance gate. A disqualified film is worth $0 regardless of quality.

Edwin holds the spine

Phedon may change any shot. He may not lose the feeling those shots exist to produce.

Braintrust speaks last

Nothing is called finished until it has been attacked. Candor above comfort — but it must ship, not merely object.

Five seats · wargaming the room before it judges us

The Jury Room

Each seat argues the way its patron judges, so an internal review rehearses the real one. Below each: the frame plates that carry that juror’s signature, the work worth watching, and the specific things that make them vote no. Full protocol in docs/10-wargame.md.

Why these are drawings, not posters

Real posters are copyrighted works and real stills are somebody’s photograph — the same rule our film has to clear governs this page. Each plate is drawn from scratch to isolate the part of a reference that is actually transferable: where the key is, where the horizon sits, what the palette does at the turn. Watch the real films at the links; steal the decisions from here.

They do not want the same film

A beautiful, static, contemplative short wins Edwin and loses Anderson outright. The winner carries feeling through momentum — that is the whole design constraint.

Anderson carries the most weight

Platform and social engagement together are 30% — more than any single craft criterion. Treating his seat as marketing forfeits the largest block on the board.

Unanimity is a red flag

Five agreeing seats usually means the concept is invisible, or four of them stopped doing their jobs. Braintrust has to say which.
