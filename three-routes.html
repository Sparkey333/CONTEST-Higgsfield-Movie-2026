Simple

Chain forward off the last frame

Never make another still. Take the last frame of a clip you already have, feed it in as the start image of the next generation, and keep walking the film forward until you hit five minutes. Prequels work the same way in reverse: your first frame becomes someone else's end image.

  1. Pick the strongest clip you own and call it the spine.
  2. Grab its last frame. Any player will export one; Cinema Studio will too.
  3. New generation, that frame as start image, one sentence of motion. Repeat.
  4. For a prequel, use your opening frame as the end image and generate backwards.
  5. Assemble in order. Deflicker, upscale, grade, out.

This is the only route that can be finished in a day.

Medium

Keep the eleven, anchor only the twelve

The eleven concept clips stay. They already cover 146 seconds and they were generated from the real shot list, so they are in the right order and pointed the right way. Spend the budget on the twelve shots that have nothing — and on the joins.

  1. Upscale the eleven you have. They are 720p; the finish is 4K.
  2. Generate a still for each end of the twelve missing shots only. Not 39 anchors, 24.
  3. Render those twelve as a start frame to end frame move, at final quality.
  4. Where a kept clip meets a new one, pull the kept clip's frame and generate into it — so the seam is made once and cannot drift.
  5. Assemble, deflicker, upscale, grade.

This is the bible's method applied only where the film is actually missing.

Advanced

The director's bible, start to finish

Nothing becomes video until the whole film exists as stills. Thirty-nine anchor frames in film order, then every shot is a move between two frames that are already right, then five gates that each stop the work below them.

  1. Gate A, identity. Lock the handles and Souls. Everything downstream inherits them.
  2. 39 anchors, in film order, chained so shots share frames across scene boundaries.
  3. Gate B, string. Watch all 39 as a stills reel before a single second is rendered.
  4. 23 shots, each a start-to-end move, by movement.
  5. Gates C, D, E — timing, motion, picture. Deflicker, upscale, grade, submit.

It is the right method. It is also twenty unheld steps and five human decisions.

The number that moved

The full method was preflighted at 1,889 credits against a balance of 2,299 — 410 of head-room. The balance is now 1,735. Route three no longer fits in one clean pass, and that is before a single re-roll. It is not a reason to abandon the method; it is a reason to stop planning as though the whole film gets the method.

What I would do with 5.8 days

Route two, with route one held in reserve. It costs 49 credits more than the cheapest option and it is the only one of the three that protects the thing the film is actually good at — twenty-three long held shots that were written to sit next to each other in a specific order.

Route one is not a lesser film, it is a different film: chaining forward is how you get five continuous minutes that no longer have a shot list underneath them. Keep it as the answer to "it is Friday and four shots are still missing", because on Friday it will be the right answer.

Route three stays the reference. It is what the next film gets when there are three weeks instead of six days — and every asset built for it is already sitting in the account, which is exactly why route two is cheap.
